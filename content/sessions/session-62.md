# Session 62 — Heads Will Roll
<!-- cover: images/S62 The Square.webp -->

## Session Overview

- **Party Level:** 12 (Karusa 13)
- **Location:** the house on the Ring → through the portal, Before the Usurpation → Amphiale → the Pitfall and the keeper's hall → Ithake → the Bloomhollow → the Middle → the great forest → Epidaure → Pergame
- **Party Members:** Farkas, Veezara, Malcius (new), plus Karusa
- **PC Deaths:** Malcius, beheaded in Pergame; he woke on the Ring in his own time
- **Theme:** A rude new elf falls out of the sky outside the house, and the four of them step through the portal into the world as it was before the Usurpation. Farkas finds his own blood in a giant who keeps the dead, a king and a grove both shrug at the end of the world, and the Middle nearly kills three of them. Then a healer gathering herbs near Epidaure turns out to be called Talaris, and Pergame takes a head for him.
- **Date:** ~late Gorravane, 2,836 AS, carried forward; many days pass on the far side of the portal
- **XP:** Farkas, Veezara and Malcius +1,750 each

## Session Log

### Morning in the house; Malcius comes down

<img src="images/S62 Malcius.webp" alt="Malcius, an elf with slicked-back platinum hair in a scorched charcoal coat over red" class="content-image">

The company woke in the house on the Ring after the long rest, with the portal standing in the lane outside. Out in the lane the air cracked like a dropped plate and Malcius, an elf pyromancer of the Bastion of Fireblood, landed hard on the cobbles a few paces from the door. He had platinum hair slicked back and a scorched charcoal coat over red, with brass vials at his belt and smoke coming off his cuffs. The last of a scroll of *teleport* was burning to grey flakes in his hand.

<img src="images/S62 The Portal.webp" alt="A door-sized gap of bright light hanging in a village lane, the lane whole and lived in on the far side" class="content-image">

The portal stood in the lane between the house and the seam, a door-sized gap hanging in the air with edges hard and bright like the seam's. Through it was the same lane, whole and lived in, with doors on the houses and smoke going up from a chimney, under a morning light from a slightly different sky. A cold, clean wind came out of it.

Malcius met the company in the lane and insulted them from the start, calling them names like "ashtray" and "lizard". Karusa came to the doorway and watched him.

### Through the portal

Despite all that, Malcius joined them. He did it only because Veezara told him that, strange as it was, he didn't want to see him hurt crossing the portal alone. Malcius, Farkas, Veezara and Karusa stepped through together. The cold wind hit them in the face for one step, and on the next it was at their backs.

<img src="images/S62 Amphiale.webp" alt="A whole fishing village of white stone houses across a narrow green finger of land, boats on both shores" class="content-image">

They came out in the same lane of the same village, but whole: two dozen low houses of white stone with slate roofs, every door on its hinges, smoke from the chimneys, the well at the crossing, nets drying on a green, goats on the slope and boats drawn up on both shores. There was no seam. The finger ran on south, straight and green, and the sea off both coasts was ordinary water. Behind them the portal stood in the lane on this side too, and through it the ruined lane of their own world. Nobody on this side could see it.

The villagers saw four armed strangers standing in their lane who hadn't come up the road, and stopped and stared. The house on the seam was a lived-in cottage. A net-maker stood at its door and sent her son Wilfrid running back inside, then told the strangers to keep away from her door, because there were children in the lane. Someone ran to fetch the eldest, Godwin, who came up the lane on his stick.

<img src="images/S62 Godwin.webp" alt="Godwin, an old man with a white beard and a stick, in a sealskin cape over grey wool, by a well" class="content-image">

The company asked Godwin what this place was. He told them it was **Amphiale**, the village on the Ring, on the Hand, and found it strange that anyone could stand in it and not know. She had drunk at this well some days ago, walking west toward the Middle, he said, and nobody had seen her since. They told him they had come through a portal. He brushed it off as a riddle, cared only that they did no harm in the village, and asked them to send word to Amphiale if they saw her on their way.

Godwin let Veezara look around the village, so long as he kept from the houses and minded the children and the goats. Tonight was a feast day in her name. It took less than an hour to walk all of Amphiale. A driftwood heap at the top of the slope grew to the height of a man, women threaded greenery into long ropes, and someone was baking in quantity. Wilfrid followed them round at a distance, turning a string of shells in his fingers.

<img src="images/S62 Wilfrid's Painting.webp" alt="A child's crayon drawing of Farkas with his maul, a purple pattern on one arm, mountains and a yellow sun" class="content-image">

Then Wilfrid came up to Farkas and handed him a painting he had made for him.

### North to the Pitfall

<img src="images/S62 Canopy Maw.webp" alt="A huge creature with wings of long bare finger-bones flying low over a herd of shaggy grazers under oaks" class="content-image">

The company left Amphiale around midday and went north toward the Pitfall. At the root of the Middle there was only grass and wind. Under the Palm's oaks a herd of shaggy grey-brown grazers the size of draught horses, thousands strong, took the better part of ten minutes to cross a glade ahead of them. Far off over the trees a Canopy Maw, slung under wings of long bare finger-bones, flew low over another herd, and didn't come their way.

<img src="images/S62 The Pitfall.webp" alt="A vast forested bowl with a waterfall going over its lip into white mist, a stone hall standing on the rim" class="content-image">

They came out of the oaks at the rim of the Pitfall. The river went over the lip into clean white mist, and far below lay the white bones of the giants: ribs like overturned hulls, and a man-shaped skull the size of a house on its side among the rocks. The mist smelled only of water. On a spur of rock along the rim stood a hall of grey stone with smoke going up from its chimney. Its doorway was taller than three Farkases, and the steps up to it came to his knee.

<img src="images/S62 Tharra.webp" alt="Tharra Long-Watch, a grey-haired giantess in a frayed undyed mantle, seated on a stone at the edge of the pit" class="content-image">

Its keeper, **Tharra Long-Watch**, a full giant of the western kingdoms, sat on a stone block beside it looking down over the pit, speaking under her breath in a deep old tongue that sounded like stones being set down one on another. She greeted them, told them she kept the dead below for the giants' kingdoms, and asked them to tread softly by the rim. She noticed Farkas's size and the giant-make of his maul.

The bones, she told Farkas, were giants of the first age, made before any of the peoples. The kingdoms of the west hold them for their first fathers and mothers, and keep one of their own blood always beside them so the dead are never left alone; she had sat with them eleven winters. She told him of the giants' kingdoms on the western high plains, far across the world: stone cities built at giant scale, storm-craft over the plains, and marrow-iron forges that never go cold, where work like his maul was made for one blood and answers to it. There was no one ruler of the west. Each kingdom had its own king and warleaders, and hers was ruled by **King Hrothgar**, who had sent her here and given her his own cloak for it.

Tharra knew no one called Yrga. She asked to see the marks on the maul, and Farkas gave it to her. In her hand it glowed, a pale steady light coming up from inside the metal. The clan-marks under its head, worn almost smooth, were the marks of her own line, and it glowed for her because she shares its blood. She had never seen this maul, and it was worn as if carried for thousands of years. She told Farkas he was of her blood.

Farkas told her he believed they had travelled to the past, and then told her the whole story as he knew it: the years between, the Sovereigns, the western kingdoms broken and the giants fled east to Thurr'gath, the maul carried down the line to Yrga and to him. Tharra believed him. She asked who had sat with the dead after the kingdoms fell. Farkas told her no one had. She said she would sit with them the harder while she was there, and as evening came on she asked her kinsman and those with him to stay the night.

### A night in the keeper's hall

The company long rested on her floor by a fire she fed with whole logs. In the morning Tharra was already outside on her stone, speaking to the dead in the old tongue, and a Canopy Maw hung asleep in a great oak along the rim, fed, its fingers trailing to the ground.

Veezara asked Tharra where he could see the stars. The rim itself on a clear night, she said, with nothing between him and the sky, or the meadow on the Middle above the sea, and he was welcome to her stone after dark. Farkas asked where he could find other giants. None of her people were on the Hand, she told him; they were in the western kingdoms, months away at his stride. On the Palm there were only the old walkers, the stone-grown giants who keep the wood. They are older than her kingdoms and seldom speak, and she didn't know who set them to their work.

### East to Ithake

Tharra walked them as far as the sleeping Maw's oak and asked Farkas to come back to the rim before they went home. They went south across the Palm and east along the shore to the Little, and down its paved road among the petitioners walking to the king, most of them clutching a scroll, a wax tablet or a bundle of tally-sticks. An old woman going to petition about a boundary stone shared their road and her bread. A figure of black iron eight feet tall, with a blank plate for a face and a bronze-strapped chest on its back, passed them walking toward the palace, and the old woman nodded to it like a neighbour.

<img src="images/S62 Ithake.webp" alt="A walled town of pale stone and red-tiled roofs climbing a narrow finger of land to a white palace above the sea" class="content-image">

They came to **Ithake**: a walled town of pale stone and tiled roofs climbing the finger in terraces to the white palace at its tip. At the palace gate the petitioners waited in a line kept by the king's steward, Joscelin, while the captain of the guard stood at the gate. Anyone in the kingdom might speak with the king in their turn, the captain told them, and the steward told them to wait it like everyone else. Some forty people were ahead of them.

<img src="images/S62 Joscelin.webp" alt="Joscelin, a grey-bearded steward in a dark green robe with keys and wax tablets" class="content-image">

Veezara persuaded Joscelin to move them up the list (Persuasion 23), and within the hour they were brought down a long hall of pillars hung with green banners into the throne room. It had no back wall: three tall arches opened straight onto the sky and the sea. King Edric sat forward on his throne, a broad, heavy man of about fifty with a close-trimmed grey beard, a plain gold crown and robes of deep green. His daughter Rohese sat on a stool at the side of the dais, watching them.

<img src="images/S62 Edric.webp" alt="King Edric of Ithake, a broad grey-bearded man in a gold crown and green robes, listening on his throne" class="content-image">

Veezara told King Edric they came from a different era, and asked him to point them to someone who could commune with her, perhaps a Greenwarder. Edric repeated the petition back, as is his way. No one communes with her, he said. She walks among her people and speaks to whom she will; he had spoken with her three times in his life, and each time she found him. She had been missing some days and no one knew where. If anyone could find her, it was the Greenwarders of the Bloomhollow, north on the Palm.

Veezara told the king about the Usurpation, the end of the world as he knew it. King Edric answered that such a thing could only come about by her will, and that it is what it is. He gave them leave and called the next petitioner.

<img src="images/S62 Rohese.webp" alt="Rohese, a young woman with a black fringe and a circlet of gold leaves, in a green gown" class="content-image">

As they went back down the pillared hall, Rohese came after them and asked whether there was an Ithake in their world. Veezara told her no: there was nothing there but death. She asked what became of her father. Veezara told her about the whole world as he knew it. She was deeply shaken. She backed away from them and went back toward the throne room alone, and stood at its doors with her forehead against the bronze.

### A night in Ithake

They found an inn two terraces below the palace, a low white house with a vine over the door. The innkeeper weighed their gold on a little bronze balance rather than counting it and took it by weight. Farkas had the floor of the larger room, because no bed would hold him.

### The grove's southern end

<img src="images/S62 Grove-Folk.webp" alt="Tiny winged grove-folk, a hand tall, gathered round a gold fruit in the hollow of a pale tree" class="content-image">

The company went back west along the shore and north up the Palm, past the Pitfall, to where the Red Wood stands in their own world. Here there was no red. The grove ran on to its southern end in pale-barked trees in green leaf, with no eyes on any of them, and the pond where Viktor died lay clear, green leaves floating on it. Small winged grove-folk, a hand tall, watched them from the trees and were gone when looked at. The great hollow tree was only a tree, with a dry hollow full of fallen leaves.

<img src="images/S62 Aldwyn.webp" alt="Aldwyn, a lean grey-blond forester in a coat of pale bark-scales with a bow of living branch" class="content-image">

A forester in a coat of pale bark-scales, with a bow of living branch still in leaf at its tips, came quickly through the trees from the south and stopped dead when he saw them. The company told him they wished to see his sister, so that she might help them find Aedwynn. He asked how strangers knew he had a sister. He had searched the Middle and the Ring's road and found nothing, he said, and was going to Briallen in the grove anyway; they could walk with him. They told him they knew of his sister through the king and introduced themselves, and he gave his name, Aldwyn.

<img src="images/S62 The Bloomhollow.webp" alt="The Bloomhollow: huge pale trunks, ferns and a clear stream in slanting light" class="content-image">

He walked them north to the Bloomhollow. The trees grew enormous, pale trunks wider than houses with walkways of lashed wood running between them high up and a clear stream below. Among the Greenwarders at their work the physician Elfrida met Aldwyn and scolded him for being gone so long. Briallen came out of the inner wood to meet her brother, and turned to the strangers he had brought.

<img src="images/S62 Aldwyn and Briallen.webp" alt="Aldwyn and his sister Briallen side by side in the grove" class="content-image">

The company asked Briallen whether they could speak with the Maker. Briallen knelt among the roots of the biggest tree by the stream, laid both hands flat on the moss and closed her eyes. She tried for a long time and couldn't find her anywhere. Aldwyn took it hard. Veezara warned Briallen of the Usurpation to come, and said it might already be too late. She heard him out. The Maker was not lost or harmed, she judged: she simply did not wish to be seen. Aldwyn accepted it too. The last anyone had seen of her, Briallen told them, she was going to the Middle. The company set out for the Middle.

### The Middle

<img src="images/S62 The Middle Root.webp" alt="A forest gone mad with growing, huge young trees and flowers the size of carts above the sea" class="content-image">

They went south from the Bloomhollow down the Palm and out onto the Middle, the longest finger. The root was a forest gone mad with growing: trees too tall and too young, flowers the size of carts, every animal carrying or dropping young. The middle reach would not hold still, its green hills going grey and back while they watched, and toward the tip the illness was winning. Death pulsed through it in waves, and only the company felt it. Around them nothing else noticed.

<img src="images/S62 The Middle Tip.webp" alt="The end of a finger of land overgrown with warm swollen knots and weeping burls above a slow grey sea" class="content-image">

At the tip the ground swelled in warm knots over everything, the burls on the trees wept something slow and clear, and the sea off the cliffs moved thick and slow. Every minute they stayed, the pulse of death called for a Constitution saving throw (DC 18); a failure dropped a creature unconscious for a minute and left it a level of exhaustion. They stood at the tip by the wound long enough to fail several, then turned back, Veezara at 5 levels of exhaustion, Karusa at 3, Malcius at 2 and Farkas at none. Off the tip, the saves stopped.

### Back to the Bloomhollow

<img src="images/S62 The Greenwarder Physician.webp" alt="Elfrida, a Greenwarder physician in a bark-scale coat with moss-green gloves and braided grey-streaked hair" class="content-image">

Back in the Bloomhollow Elfrida met them and took charge of the worst of them. Veezara was cold as a stone, and she knew dragonborn run hot. She could find no cause: Aldwyn had walked the Middle end to end for days and come back whole. Briallen gave them a place to rest on the platforms, put her hands into the stream and looked down the Middle to where they had been, and to her sight the land there was still green. The company long rested in the Bloomhollow, and greater restorations were cast on them, which cleared all their exhaustion.

### West through the forest

<img src="images/S62 The Great Forest.webp" alt="An old forest of huge oak and beech with a worn track running through it" class="content-image">

They set out west from the Bloomhollow toward where the Blooming Rot stands in their world, through a great forest that runs unbroken from the grove westward: oak and beech grown huge, moss on everything. On the first day a tall woman on a dark horse watched them pass from the edge of the trees and rode off.

<img src="images/S62 The Woman on the Dark Horse.webp" alt="A tall pale woman in a heavy black mantle standing in a night forest" class="content-image">

On the second night, camped alone among the trees, the company slept and Malcius kept the watch alone. The frogs went quiet. The woman, Adelais, came into the firelight and spoke to him, with two others waiting at the edge of the light. A fight followed. She bit Veezara, kissed him, and turned to mist as she left. The company killed the two who had come with her. After a while the frogs started up again.

### Epidaure

The company went back to the Bloomhollow to have Veezara checked; the Greenwarders found nothing wrong. Then they took the road west again through the forest.

<img src="images/S62 Epidaure.webp" alt="Long low timber halls among gardens and a stone bridge over a stream in a forest clearing" class="content-image">

They came to **Epidaure**: long low timber halls with steep shingled roofs and deep porches in clearings of the forest, gardens of flowers and herbs in stone-edged beds, a stream with a stone bridge, and mossed graves under the old oaks.

<img src="images/S62 Talaris.webp" alt="A broad grey-bearded man in an embroidered linen coat kneeling in a forest clearing, picking herbs into a leather satchel" class="content-image">

In the woods near the town a man was collecting herbs to make medicine. The company asked him who he was. He told them he was Talaris, Epidaure's physician. The name shocked them, but he seemed far too different from the Talaris they knew.

Malcius wanted vengeance on Talaris, because in the company's own time Talaris is at war with the Bastion of Fireblood. After a moral debate, Veezara agreed that Malcius should attack him. Veezara watched as Malcius incinerated Talaris with *fireball*. As he fell, Talaris could only manage two things, "Why...?" and "Alys, take the kids and run...", before he burned to death.

His wife Alys and their children ran. As the company went on through Epidaure, the people who had seen what they did hid from them and fled.

### To Pergame

<img src="images/S62 Pergame.webp" alt="A great river city of red-tiled roofs along a wide slow river crossed by stone bridges" class="content-image">

The company left Epidaure and went west and north out of the forest onto the great river plains, to where Veinspire stands in their world. It was the most crowded country they had seen on either side of the portal, farms and villages close together, each with a well and a shrine with her name cut over it. The city there is **Pergame**, the largest in the world, on a great slow river: four great stone bridges, markets, a thousand red-tiled roofs, and its houses of healing along the waterfront. The wind off it smelled of woodsmoke, bread, river water and something sharp, like herbs boiled in vinegar. They crossed the river by the nearest bridge.

### The church of Pergame

<img src="images/S62 The Church.webp" alt="An old ivy-covered church of grey stone on a terrace above a river city, veiled stone figures and candles in niches along its wall" class="content-image">

The company went to the only church in Pergame, on a stone terrace at the top of the ridge above the river. It was grey stone with ivy up its walls and no tower. Along its outer wall stood stone figures of a veiled woman in niches, with candles, flowers and bread at their feet, and from inside came many voices saying the same words over and over: *She see me. She keep me. She walk by.*

<img src="images/S62 The Young Guard.webp" alt="A young woman guard in mail under a red mantle, a long sword over her shoulder, on a terrace above the city" class="content-image">

Among its guards was a young woman in mail under a red mantle, a long sword across her back. The company gave their weapons to her at the door; Farkas's maul nearly pulled her arms out of their sockets. Veezara showed her the torn badge from the house on the Ring. It meant nothing to her.

Inside was one long hall of grey stone on plain round pillars, with no benches and no altar, only a plain stone step and, cut into the wall behind it, a veiled woman walking. A priest was preaching to a full church. The Maker made the world and walks in it, he said; she asks and listens, and never asked to be worshipped. This week she has gone where no one can find her, and she owes no one an account of where she walks. The faithful should do as she does and look at things properly, the sick and the poor first.

After the sermon, Malcius asked a nun whether she knew anyone by the name of Mareatha. She said she didn't. He was extremely rude and condescending to her about it, and the priest grew impatient with him. Malcius noticed and left the nun alone.

### The guard comes for Malcius

The company went to an inn in Pergame and long rested. In the morning the young guard knocked at Malcius's door with two more behind her. Word had come up the road with the people fleeing Epidaure: a smoking elf among four armed strangers had burned the physician Talaris to death. She took Malcius to a guardhouse by the second bridge to answer for the killing of an innocent man.

### Before the council of eight

<img src="images/S62 Maren Thane.webp" alt="Maren Thane, a tall gaunt woman with long black hair in a charcoal mantle" class="content-image">

Malcius was brought before Pergame's council of eight, in a long hall under the great dome with eight carved chairs at the far end. Maren Thane read Malcius's mind and Veezara's, and saw the burning of Talaris, with Veezara standing by while it was done. On her word the council had Veezara arrested as well. The council sentenced Malcius to death. For standing by while it was done, it set Veezara a blood-price of 1,000 gp in gold, to be paid to Talaris's widow Alys and her children Ralf and Amice, and banished him from the heartland. If he is found in Pergame or its lands again, he answers as Malcius did.

<img src="images/S62 The Square.webp" alt="A rain-wet market square in front of a running stone fountain, with a timber guillotine on a raised platform" class="content-image">

Veezara paid the blood-price, weighed out on the city's scales. Malcius was taken to the guillotine in the square before the fountain and executed. The fountain went on running.

### Malcius wakes

<img src="images/S61 The Ring.webp" alt="A green finger of land broken by a seam of white light, one white house standing near it" class="content-image">

Malcius woke outside the house on the Ring, in the ruined lane of his own world, with the portal standing in the lane. The door of the house still lay flat, the seam hung bright beyond it, and the far half of the Ring lay tipped on its side. His things stayed on the far side, because they had been taken from him when he was arrested. The young guard gave them to Veezara, since they were all that remained of him: his potions and a magic item.

### A long rest, apart

Malcius long rested in the empty house on the Ring. On the far side, the watch saw Veezara, Farkas and Karusa out through Pergame's gates, and the three long rested outside the walls before setting out of the heartland.

Malcius is beside the portal on the Ring, in his own world, after a long rest. Veezara, Farkas and Karusa are outside Pergame's gates on the far side, after a long rest, about to leave the heartland. Veezara is banished from it.
