# Session 59: Gulm Eat First
<!-- cover: images/S59 The Still Pond.webp -->

## Session Overview

- **Party Level:** 12
- **Location:** Crownfall → out the East Gate across the dead land → a pond at the first camp → the coast and a white door in the cliff → the arm of land and the bridge at the neck → the shade → the Shadow Quay → the Blackfold
- **Party Members:** Farkas, Veezara, Viktor, plus Karusa
- **PC Deaths:** None
- **Theme:** The company walks from Crownfall to the Umbrasseur Isles, pulls a smiling dead man out of a pond that won't ripple, pays a toll in rations to a thing on a bridge, and spends its first day in a city where the shadows have rights.
- **Date:** ~late Gorravane, 2,836 AS
- **XP:** Farkas +6,500; Veezara and Viktor +4,000 each

---

## Session Log

### Morning in Crownfall

The company woke at the inn in Crownfall after the long rest. Viktor still had one level of exhaustion. They were going to the Umbrasseur Isles on foot.

Karusa wore her hat of disguise for the whole trip. She only took it off to sleep, on the road or at an inn.

Before they left, Veezara bought four Potions of Greater Healing. They left Crownfall that morning.

### Out the East Gate

They went out through Crownfall's East Gate. Past the wall the land was grey and dead to every horizon, cut by ridges of exposed bone with scrub growing in the cracks, and now and then the wind brought ash up from the Burn. There was no road. They kept the city at their backs and walked south-east for the coast.

In the afternoon they spotted a pack of Cindermaws a long way off: low black shapes with orange light glowing through the cracks in their bodies, crossing between two bone ridges. The pack kept its distance and never came near the company.

### The Pond

<img src="images/S59 The Still Pond.webp" alt="A small round pond on a grey dead plain at dusk, mirror-still, a stone resting on it without a ripple" class="content-image">

They made camp for the first night beside a small pond. When something was dropped into it, the water didn't ripple. Nobody could make sense of that.

Veezara asked Karusa to come and take a look, since they were closer to the Umbrasseur Isles now and she might know what it was. Karusa walked to the pond and put her feet in the water, as if something had entranced her. Veezara burned the water away with his breath. Once it was gone Karusa came out of the trance. She looked confused, as if she hadn't realised what was happening to her.

Then Veezara spotted a lump in the ground where the pond had been. He dug at it, found a hand, and pulled up the body of a man. The man was dead, and he was smiling. He wore a locket, and from it Veezara worked out that he came from the Bastion and had been through heartbreak: half of the locket, where his partner would be, had been scratched out. Veezara kept the locket.

### The Night

That night they could see the Cindermaws' glow out in the dark. Sometime after midnight the pack seemed frightened by something. It broke up and ran, the glows going off fast in different directions. Nobody saw what spooked them.

They kept watch for the rest of the night, and in the morning they went on toward the coast.

### The White Door

The ground got rockier and sloped down, and there was salt on the wind. Then the dead plain stopped at a line of grey cliffs, with grey water below and surf breaking white on the rocks.

They climbed down to the rocky beach and went up a worn path to a door set flush into the cliff, limewashed so white you only saw its edges standing right in front of it. A voice from inside told them to go away. They kept pushing and opened the door anyway.

<img src="images/S59 The Man Behind the White Door.webp" alt="A man cased head to foot in white lime, with clusters of grey hands where his own hands should be" class="content-image">

The smell came first, sharp and chalky like a builder's lime pit, with something sweeter and rotten under it. Inside was one big chamber. Four round stone basins of milky water were sunk into the wet grey floor, and a few iron lanterns in the corners were turned down low. Sackcloth bundles the size of people hung from a rope along the far wall, dripping white, and their shadows on the wall behind them stood upright. On a ledge, hundreds of cut-off soles of feet lay in rows, face up, each with a number on it. In the middle of the room, in a frame of lashed splints, something twice a man's height was being sewn together from grey bodies with white hair for thread, and loose hands along its seams were still pushing a needle through.

The man who lived there was cased head to foot in white lime, smooth even over his face apart from one slot for the eyes, and each of his wrists ended in a bunch of grey hands, all moving. He decided they weren't there to harm him and let his guard down. When they asked who he was, he said it was none of their business. All he wanted was more bodies, he said, and his brethren would have their vengeance soon. That confused the company, but they didn't pry much further. They did make out that he was a Shadar-Kai.

They asked him a few questions about the Isles, then left and went on with their walk.

### The Bridge at the Neck

They followed the coast south until the land narrowed into a long arm running down toward the Isles, with the inlet's grey water on their right. On their left the scrub got thicker and warmer underfoot the further they went, toward the Bastion. There was no road and no sign that anyone lived there.

<img src="images/S59 Gulm.webp" alt="Gulm, a squat barrel-bodied creature crouched across a stone bridge, one huge arm and one long thin one" class="content-image">

Where the arm narrows, a deep, dry fissure cuts across it from the inlet almost to the eastern shore, and now and then a stretch of its wall twitched like a muscle. One old stone bridge crosses it. At its north end lay a heap of picked grey bones and empty feed-sacks, some of them years old.

Gulm had plopped himself down in the middle of the bridge. He was squat and barrel-bodied and exactly as wide as the bridge, crouched with a knee and a set of huge knuckles on each parapet. He had no neck, just a broad flat jaw across his whole front with four small dark eyes set wide above it. His left arm was enormous. His right was long and thin, and its hand lay open on the bridge in front of him, palm up. When he talked, his voice was small and mild, like an ordinary man's.

> “Food? You got food?”<br>“Gulm eat first. Then you walk.”<br>— Gulm

He wouldn't let them pass without it. Viktor tried *modify memory* on him. It angered Gulm, who attacked Viktor, and the fight started. Veezara tried to walk past Gulm and was flung back.

In the end it was Karusa, with her sharp eyes, who found the way across. She handed Gulm a ration, and he let her pass. The others did the same, and Gulm told them there was no need for violence and that he doesn't like violence.

Farkas didn't cross. He couldn't accept the defeat, and since he hadn't finished the fight he felt he hadn't earned the crossing. He went round by the coast instead. On the way he fought rift crawlers, the coast's crabs, and beat them, then came in from the west and rejoined the company on the far side.

### Into the Shade

They rested, then went on south together. A day further on, the light stopped. It didn't fade. Behind them the sky was lit as usual, and ahead was a soft grey dusk that stayed the same however long they walked in it.

At the end of the land, black basalt pylons stood at the water's edge, and from them a bridge of solid shadow ran out over slate-coloured water to the first islands of the city. A crew was working on it.

The guards at the pylons looked the company over and sent them across in single file, not side by side, to register at the Shadow Quay.

### The Shadow Quay

<img src="images/S59 Shadow Quay.webp" alt="The Shadow Quay: black piers on slate water under a grey dusk, each ship's shadow falling a different way" class="content-image">

The span brought them down onto a harbour that reflected no lamps: long black piers on slate water, and every moored ship throwing its shadow in a different direction. Porters worked without talking under cranes whose counterweights started rising before the loads came down.

They were sent to the first desk at the foot of the piers. The clerk there was a lean man in a severe black coat with the pale, ordinary face of a middle-aged clerk. As he wrote their arrival into his ledger, his shadow on the wall behind him wrote along with him, with a stylus of its own. He explained the visitor bond before they agreed to anything: a paper token, and a mark that goes on the shadow.

They took the bond, gave the Grey Pot as their lodging, and crossed by boat into the Blackfold. Karusa didn't declare her hat's disguise. So many people in the city wear faces that nobody questioned it.

### The Grey Pot

<img src="images/S59 Grey Pot.webp" alt="The Grey Pot: a low black-timbered inn with covered windows and a lamp over the door" class="content-image">

The boat put them ashore among black piers in the same grey dusk. The streets were narrow and busy, and every shadow in them leaned the same way, toward the middle of the city, wherever the lamps were. Every shop window that might have shown a reflection had dark cloth over it. On one corner a woman slapped a little boy's hand for stepping on a stranger's shadow, and the stranger nodded at her as if she'd done the right thing.

Three streets from the quay they found the Grey Pot, a low black-timbered building with its windows covered and a lamp over the door. Inside it was dark and low-ceilinged. Everything that might shine was covered in dark linen: the mirror behind the bar, the brass on the taps, even the backs of the spoons on the tables. The place smelled of stew, thick and salty with something sweet under it, from a pot kept warm behind the counter.

The innkeeper, a big, soft-spoken man in the face of an old half-elf, with fine cracks showing at the corners of his eyes, gave them rooms. They long rested, and Viktor's exhaustion cleared.

During the rest, Veezara asked Karusa about her past and where she came from. She didn't tell him much. While they talked, Veezara noticed that her shadow was holding its head and shaking. He asked her if she'd like to visit his home some day, and she said she looked forward to it.

### The Reading Room

Morning in the Blackfold only shows in the bells. One rang somewhere out over the water, then another further off, and the grey outside the covered windows looked just as it had the night before. Downstairs the innkeeper had the pot on and fresh bread out, and offered to send for Ribhaven fish for anyone who didn't want food from the pot.

Viktor wanted to find a library. The city has none. People there don't write down more than they have to, the innkeeper said, but there is a small private reading room for foreigners in the Gray Market, with books off the Ribhaven boats, sea charts and law. He sent Viktor west over the bridges to look for the sign with a shadow like an open book.

The way west crossed two bridges of solid shadow, and at the second they waited while a bell rang and the whole span folded up out of the way for a ferry. Past it the Gray Market's streets ran into narrow arcades under layers of awnings. Every shop hung its sign so the lamplight threw a shadow of it onto the street, and you read the shadows rather than the signs: a boot, a key, a pair of scissors. The open book was down a side arcade. Inside was one long, quiet room with shelves to the ceiling and reading tables down the middle. The lamps were fixed low along the walls so that a reader's shadow fell behind him and never across the page, and the one mirror, by the door, was covered. The keeper, a woman in a plain grey dress, sat at a desk by the door with a ledger. Two silver to sit for the day, name and bond number in the book, and nothing leaves the room.

Farkas went looking for somewhere to fight. Next door to the reading room was the Last Lamp, a shop that sells hooded lanterns. The lantern-seller, trimming wicks, didn't seem surprised by the question. He pointed Farkas to the Shadow Pits, under the lamp-oil warehouse at the end of the row, and told him it isn't the man who fights there but his shadow. You stand on a step without moving your feet, your shadow goes down onto the white floor to fight the other man's, and whatever it takes, you take. Bouts are at night, with purses on the undercard and bigger ones later, and everybody goes to watch the old Shadar-Kai who never moves and has been winning for eleven years.

Viktor asked the keeper for a book on shadows, and Veezara for one on the Isles' history, mainly its notable events. She brought Viktor *The Visitor's Shadow: A Plain Account of the Law*, the thin black book ship's people read so they don't get fined, and gave Veezara *An Account of the Shadowed Isles, for Masters and Pursers Trading There*, a fat, salt-stained book by a Ribhaven trader. What the two books said is written up in Shadow Law and the Isles' History.

Viktor asked for books on a cult of Qhal'nimeth. The keeper wrote the name down the way she heard it, ran a finger down her shelf list and shook her head. They had nothing, and she'd never heard of it.

### The Keeper's House

Viktor thanked her and went to the Mirror Quarter to look for a "healing ward". The quarter was quieter than the Gray Market and more careful. The shop windows were smoked, the brass plates dulled, and the public basins had lids marked *by appointment*. Only outside the court buildings did mirrors stand uncovered, tall ones in iron frames with clerks beside them. In a small square the crowd gave a wide berth to a man holding up a mirror on a pole, and nobody looked into it.

<img src="images/S59 The Keeper.webp" alt="The Keeper, a tired carer in a linen apron with his sleeves rolled up" class="content-image">

A court clerk sent Viktor two streets back from the court to a narrow, tall house with a green door, smoked windows and a dulled brass plate: *Registered Convalescent Residence: Four Beds*. Viktor knocked. The Keeper, a man, answered the door with his sleeves rolled to the elbow and a linen apron over his clothes, his hands red from hot water. He had the steady tiredness of someone who's been tired for a long time.

Viktor told him he had a rare affliction spreading over his body, purple veins, and showed him. The Keeper looked at them the way someone who's spent years with sick people looks at things. He took Viktor's wrist in two fingers, pressed gently on one of the purple lines with his thumb and watched the skin around it. Then he asked Viktor in, and warned him the second stair was loose.

He examined him properly in a small front room he clearly worked in: a scrubbed table, a basin and jug, rolled bandages, stoppered bottles with handwritten labels, and one good lamp. He'd never seen it before, he said. It wasn't a rash and it wasn't in the skin; it was the vessels themselves. Then, washing his hands with his back to Viktor, he asked whether Viktor heard anything with it. Viktor read him and saw nothing but a careful, tired carer.

Viktor told him that he can understand Deep Speech and has formed some sort of connection, so that he can understand aberrations and the like. The Keeper asked what the connection was like. He said he had four people upstairs who didn't talk any more. They weren't asleep and they weren't in pain, but they weren't there behind the eyes, and nobody had been able to tell him where they'd gone. He asked Viktor to come up and see if he heard anything near them.

Viktor went upstairs with him. The Keeper opened the four rooms off the landing, gently, the way you open a door on someone asleep. Each room was small and clean, with a smoked window, a bed with tight corners, and a jug of water under a cloth. In each bed a resident lay breathing slowly, eyes open on the ceiling, washed and combed, and the lamp on the landing threw each one's shadow onto the wall. The shadows weren't lying down. Three of them sat beside their beds, the way someone sits with a sick friend. The fourth stood with its back to its bed, facing the door, and it was the only one that looked worried.

So Viktor went to the fourth resident, a man, and cast *detect thoughts* on him. He heard the man's thoughts, but they came from his own crystal, the Whispering Stone, not from the man in the bed. They were only the same words, over and over:

> *“It's all right. It doesn't hurt now. It's all right. It doesn't hurt now.”*

The man had no other thoughts. Viktor told the Keeper what he had heard. The Keeper didn't say anything about it. Then Viktor took out his crystal. The Keeper looked at it for a while but kept his hands at his sides.

> “That's... a stone.”<br>— the Keeper

He asked where Viktor got it. Viktor told him it was his patron's crystal. The Keeper didn't ask who the patron was, and told him he'd want to keep it put away round here, since people look at anything shiny like it's a mirror.

Viktor spoke to the man's shadow, and it answered with its hands and body. It showed him on the world map where its owner had gone unconscious, a small island in the western sea past the Crucible of Flesh, and that a man had approached him there. The Keeper saw it and was surprised that Viktor could communicate with the unresponsive patient.

Viktor went back to the Grey Pot. Veezara went exploring the Mirror Quarter. The session ended there, on the company's first day in the Blackfold.
