# Session 65 — The Apple of His Eye
<!-- cover: images/S65 The Weeper.webp -->

## Session Overview

- **Party Level:** 14 (Anvil 11, Karusa 14)
- **Location:** the giants' steading → down a grey stair into a barred town → west to Phlegre, the Long-Watch forge and the training hall → the Small Hall → a tower in a sea → south to the last rise above the singing coast
- **Party Members:** Farkas, Veezara, Anvil, plus Karusa
- **PC Deaths:** None
- **Theme:** A hole with a grey stair opens in the floor of a giant's hall overnight. Below it is a town where every door is barred from the outside, and in the cellar of the one house that isn't, a man-shaped roach eats and cries for his mother. In the giants' city an old smith reads his own mark on Farkas's maul, a warleader laughs at a quarter giant, and a second stair opens onto a tower in an endless sea, where something huge has been eating the people one by one.
- **Date:** a winter month, carried forward
- **XP:** Farkas +9,350, Veezara +9,350, Anvil +18,700

## Session Log

### The opening in the hall

<img src="images/S65 The Opening in the Hall.webp" alt="A square frost-ringed hole with a grey stair going down in the stone floor of a giant's hall" class="content-image">

In the morning there was a square hole about six feet across in the floor of the giant's long hall, where there had been nothing the night before. Its edges were sharp and clean, like cut plaster, and the floor round it was white with frost in a ring a few yards wide. Cold air breathed up out of it, with a thin mist off the rim. A stair of smooth grey steps went down, all one colour with no joins, the same grey as the hallway in Demelza's wall. From down the stair came a faint scratching at wood, low down and in many places at once, and under it the sound of flies. The draught smelled of rotten apples.

Hereward was asleep. The company shook him awake, and he was as confused by it as they were.

### Down the stair

The company went in, Karusa with them. The giant came as far as the edge of the frost and stayed there. The stair ran down about forty feet, colder with each step, to a grey hallway about a hundred feet long that ended at a plain wall with one doorless frame. A lamp lit only half as far as it should. There was no echo, and nothing in it moved. At the frame the air coming through was damp and cool and smelled of rotten apples.

### The barred town

<img src="images/S65 The Barred Town.webp" alt="A narrow wet street of tall plastered houses at dusk, every door barred with planks" class="content-image">

Through the frame was a narrow street of tall plastered houses at dusk, with wet cobbles and nobody about. Every door was barred from the outside, with planks bolted across or iron bars in brackets, and the wood was scored with long scratches. There was a bowl on every step, empty, some of them cracked, and a soft apple in the gutter. Some windows were black with flies over shapes on the floors and beds. Others showed empty rooms: a stripped bed, a shut door, a hole gnawed through the floorboards. The scratching was under the street, low in the walls and drains, in many places at once. Further along stood the biggest house in sight, its front door shut and no bar across it.

### The unbarred house

Its front door opened. Inside, nobody was home and the rooms were as they'd been left. The smell of apples was stronger here, coming from below. A stair went up to the floor above. The cellar door stood open, and from below came chewing and a young man's voice, too low to make out the words, with the scratching louder under this house than anywhere in the street.

### The cellar

<img src="images/S65 The Weeper.webp" alt="The Weeper, a man-shaped roach with a black shell hood, long antennae and spiked arms in a brick cellar of apple barrels, a lantern at his feet" class="content-image">

The company went down into the cellar. Low brick vaults ran off in rooms joined by wide openings, with stored furniture under dust sheets, holes and cracks all along the bottoms of the walls, and a channel of brown water down the far side. At the far end, in the room with the apple barrels, the Weeper crouched over the body of a woman in good clothes, eating. He had a man's height and shape, a hood of near-black shell over his head and shoulders, a roach's head with heavy mandibles and long antennae swept back, and split wing cases hanging behind him like a ragged cloak. He had four arms, two long ones ending in serrated spikes and two shorter ones with hooked claws folded at the ribs. In the middle of his back was an old apple, shrivelled black and grown over with shell. In the holes along the walls, antennae moved in the dark.

Between mouthfuls he spoke, in a young man's frightened voice: *"Mother, where are you? I'm scared... I don't want to..."* Then he went back to eating, and the fight began.

<img src="images/S65 The Weeper, Moulted.webp" alt="The Weeper after the Moult, a pale soft roach-man stepping out of an empty dark husk in the cellar" class="content-image">

**The company killed him.** When he died, the scratching in the walls stopped.

### The apple

**Farkas ate the apple from his back**, the old shrivelled apple grown over with shell, and his Wisdom rose by 2 for good.

### The crumble

The town began to go grey and come apart, and the company ran. They went up the cellar stairs, through the empty house and out into the street while the colour went out of everything behind them. Before they reached the frame the grey took it all, and they were standing at the top of the stair in the giant's hall. Then the stair, the hallway and the hole were gone, and the frost melted off the floor. The giant was still standing where the hole had been. "You came back."

### West to Phlegre

<img src="images/S65 Phlegre.webp" alt="A giants' city of huge stone halls on a long rise above grassy plains, forge-fires glowing along its foot" class="content-image">

The company short rested at the steading, then went on west across the high plains toward the forge-glow, with wild aurochs on the grass and storms on the horizon. They reached **Phlegre**, King Hrothgar's city, on its long rise above the plains, with its marrow-iron forges along its foot.

<img src="images/S65 The Gate-Warden.webp" alt="A huge giant gate-warden with a staff standing in a great stone gateway" class="content-image">

At the gate the warden, Ecgbert Gate-Stone, stopped them: "Whose word brings you to Phlegre?" He let them through under the king's law that small folk are guests ("Small ones are guests here. That is the king's law.") and told them they'd sleep at the Small Hall, "the only door you can reach."

### The Long-Watch forge

<img src="images/S65 The Forge-Father.webp" alt="An old white-haired giant smith with burned forearms at an anvil before a glowing forge" class="content-image">

Farkas went looking for the maker of his hammer, and the company came to the Long-Watch forge. Under a crane and chains beside a banked hearth, Eadwulf Ember-Hand, the forge-father, was working at an anvil: an old giant, broad for his kind, with white hair and beard tied back and forearms burned smooth. He put his hammer down, crouched, and looked at the Skyfather's Reckoning for a long time without touching it. "That is Long-Watch work." Then: "Every mark on it is a name. Let me read you the names."

Farkas handed him the maul. In his hand it glowed, as it had in Tharra's at the Pitfall. He turned the head to the forge-light and ran his thumb along the marks. The first one he read was the hearth: "This one is the hearth. This forge." He read the rest under his breath, one by one, and stopped at his own. "That is my mark." He turned the maul over and looked at the wear on it. "My hand. My hearth." / "I never made this." He set it down on the anvil and looked at Farkas: "This is my work. But it is old. Older than me." / "Where did you get it?"

Farkas told him he got it from his mother, and nothing more. Eadwulf took that as the maul having gone down the line, which is what he makes them for: "From your mother. Then it went down the line." He gave it back. "I do not know how it got so old." / "But it is in the line's hands. That is what I make them for." He went back to his anvil, still glancing at it.

Farkas asked about Ceolric, whose name Tostig had given him at the duelling field. Ceolric Stone-Wake, Eadwulf told him, is the king's first warleader. He drills the young at first light every day, he wants a war the kingdoms won't give him, and if Farkas goes to him carrying that maul, Ceolric will want to fight him.

Farkas bought a **Forgework Dragon Shield** at the forge. In Phlegre Veezara bought a **Helm of Comprehending Languages**, and sold his Flame Tongue greatsword for 1,000 gp.

### Ceolric Stone-Wake

<img src="images/S65 Ceolric.webp" alt="Ceolric Stone-Wake, a huge scarred giant in riveted iron plate and a fur cloak with a spiked club over his shoulder" class="content-image">

The company went to meet **Ceolric Stone-Wake** at the training hall, where young giants were drilling in pairs on a sand floor, wrestling and throwing each other. Ceolric watched them with a spiked club over his shoulder, huge even for a giant, in riveted iron plate and a fur cloak, with a scar across the nose and bronze rings in his ears. He saw four small people walk in and the maul on Farkas's back, and came over. Farkas told him he was half giant. Ceolric laughed: "Maybe quarter giant."

Farkas asked Ceolric a number of questions and thanked him, and Ceolric put the club back on his shoulder and went back to his young giants.

### The Small Hall

<img src="images/S65 The Small Hall.webp" alt="A long hall of giant-built stone fitted out at human scale, with benches, barrels and lamplight" class="content-image">

The company long rested at the Small Hall.

### The tower in the sea

In the morning there was frost on the floor and cold air coming through a doorless frame that hadn't been there, a wet wind with salt in it, and the sound of gulls and of water slapping against stone far below. Behind it was a short grey hallway, with one frame at the end.

<img src="images/S65 The Tower in the Sea.webp" alt="A rough round stone tower standing alone in a grey sea, a huge eel coiled round it" class="content-image">

Through it, the company came up the head of a tower's stair onto its top floor in a cold wind: a round, rough, half-built top about a hundred feet across, with a low ring wall broken by three wide gaps, stacks of building stone, and two great olive-black coils lying over the wall. Flat grey-green sea ran to every horizon, with no land anywhere. The tower-folk, a few dozen in fish-skin coats, were gathered at the north edge round a feeding arm, where a woman stood in the net basket with her arms folded and an old man held a pouch of lots. Nobody who wasn't one of them had ever come up that stair, and they stared. Then the stones ground, the top shook, the basket swung out over the water, and the eel's head came up the wall to meet it.

<img src="images/S65 The Eel.webp" alt="A gigantic olive-black eel rising out of the sea with its jaws open beside a stone tower" class="content-image">

Farkas tried to talk to the eel, but it kept calling him a snack, and Farkas didn't like that. **The company killed the eel.** It let go of the tower and went down into the green dark, and the tower stopped shaking for the first time any of the folk could remember.

<img src="images/S65 The Keeper of the Lots.webp" alt="An old white-bearded man in a fish-skin coat leaning on the tower's ring wall above the sea" class="content-image">

The company came away with the **Black Chip**, the one blackened lot from the keeper's pouch, a soot-black chip of the tower's own stone that had sent someone down to the eel at every feeding. The old keeper of the lots thanked them plainly: "We've been climbing away from that since my grandmother's time. Thank you." Then the stone underfoot turned soft and grey, the sea went still and flat as slate, and the world came apart round them. The company was back in the Small Hall, where the frame had been. The frame was gone and the frost was melting off the boards.

### South, toward Elysra

<img src="images/S65 The Singing Coast.webp" alt="White conservatories on sea cliffs above a harbour full of masts, sea-stacks offshore" class="content-image">

The company left Phlegre and headed south, planning to go to where Elysra will be. The road ran down off the high plains toward the sea. They came over the last rise above the singing coast. Below them was a harbour full of masts, with white conservatories along the clifftops and sea-stacks offshore, and singing carried up from everywhere.

The session ended there, on the last rise.
