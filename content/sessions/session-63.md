# Session 63: Struck in Bronze
<!-- cover: images/S63 The Deep Forge.webp -->

## Session Overview

- **Party Level:** 12 (Anvil 9, Karusa 13)
- **Location:** Amphiale → Anvil's road across the Hand and the forest → Pergame's gates → the heartland's boundary stone → the road south → the duelling field → Lemnor → the coral coast → Acheront
- **Party Members:** Farkas, Veezara, Anvil (back), plus Karusa
- **PC Deaths:** None
- **Theme:** Anvil walks out of the ice and through the portal and catches the others at Pergame's gates. On the road south Veezara's powers die in his hands in the middle of a fight. Farkas beats a giant's boy in front of a field of giants, Veezara confesses at a forge under a dwarf town and strikes his oath back into bronze, and a woman called Kaedris puts him on his back. In the twilight city across the strait, a midwife writes Karusa's name on her wrist.
- **Date:** a winter month, carried forward; many days pass on the far side of the portal
- **XP:** Farkas +3,400, Veezara +3,000, Anvil +2,500

## Session Log

### Anvil through the portal

<img src="images/S63 Anvil.webp" alt="Anvil, a seven-foot warforged in dark iron plate with amber light glowing in his chest" class="content-image">

Anvil stepped through the portal in the lane outside the house on the Ring and came out in the same lane, whole: white houses and slate roofs, smoke from the chimneys, nets drying on the green, the well at the crossing. The cold, clean wind came with him. Godwin, the village's eldest, was at the well with a full bucket at his feet and saw a seven-foot warforged standing in the lane who hadn't come up the road.

Godwin came up the lane on his stick and said that this was twice now: four armed strangers had stood on the same spot some days back, saying they came through a door that wasn't there. He asked Anvil where he came from and whether he had seen Her on his way. The net-maker watched from her doorway with a half-mended net over her arm, and her boy from behind her.

### The road to Pergame

Anvil followed the company's trail. Godwin sent him north, the way the tall one with the hammer had led. At the Pitfall's rim the giant keeper by her hall knew exactly which half-giant he meant and sent him east. Down the petitioners' road in Ithake the steward remembered four strangers he had moved up his line, and in the pale woods of the grove small winged folk watched him from the branches. West through the great forest, people on the road toward Pergame talked of a smoking elf who had burned the physician at Epidaure. The road came out of the forest onto the river plains and to Pergame.

### At Pergame's gates

<img src="images/S63 Gate Watch.webp" alt="A watchman of Pergame in mail under a red cloak, a spear in hand, above the river city" class="content-image">

In the morning, after their long rest outside the walls, Farkas, Veezara and Karusa were setting off from Pergame's gates with men of the watch seeing Veezara on his way. The gate was already busy with carts and woodsmoke. Anvil came up the road at the same time. The gate sergeant dealt with him as with any warforged traveller and asked his business in Pergame.

### South to the duelling field

<img src="images/S63 The Boundary Stone.webp" alt="A carved standing stone beside a rutted road at the edge of wheat-country, poplars beyond" class="content-image">

Anvil joined the company at the gates. The four set out for the giants' duelling field by the road south. Two men of the watch walked with Veezara as far as the heartland's boundary stone at the edge of the wheat-country, a stone carved with a walking woman and the council's eight marks. One of them tapped it with his spear-butt, and they turned back there. The company went on south through the winter stubble of the lowland, then east toward the field, where the fields gave way to rough grazing and long wind-combed grass.

### The bulette

<img src="images/S63 Bulette.webp" alt="A huge armoured burrowing beast bursting up out of a dirt road beside a wrecked cart" class="content-image">

A cart lay on its side in the ditch ahead with one wheel snapped and the ground round it broken up in a ring, as if something had ploughed it from underneath. Then the road shivered underfoot, a line of grass lifted and slumped alongside them, and a ridged fin of grey-blue plate came up through the soil like a ship's keel through water. A bulette as big as a barn burst up out of the ground under the road and attacked.

**The company fought the bulette and killed it.** Partway through the fight Veezara's divine powers stopped answering him. Afterwards it lay across the broken road half in its own hole, its back plates cracked open, with a smell of turned soil and scorched horn over the furrows it had ploughed.

### The duelling field

<img src="images/S63 The Duelling Field.webp" alt="Giants fighting on a trampled plain, watched by a crowd and a line of carts along the road" class="content-image">

Where the lowland runs out against the great eastern forest, on the ground where the Eastern Bonefield will be, they came to the giants' duelling field: a plain trampled to bare earth, wide enough to drill an army on. Carts and wagons were drawn up along the road to watch, with a beer-wagon, a smith's cart stacked with bent iron and farm boys up the poplars. A bookmaker, Simkin, chalked odds on a board at a cart's tail.

Two giants about thirty-five feet tall, one bald and one shaggy, were fighting inside a ring of twenty or thirty more, insulting each other as they fought. The bald one told the shaggy one he was so fat his mother said he ate a cow and then the man who sold her the cow. The shaggy one said at least he had hair, and that a bird would sit on the bald one's head thinking it was her egg. The giants here speak simply and slowly. At the field's edge an old grey-haired giantess set bones on a bench, and a big scarred giant stood apart from the ring, waiting for someone to face him.

<img src="images/S63 The Red-Bearded Giant.webp" alt="A broad red-bearded giant laughing, a crowd of giants and carts behind him" class="content-image">

The giants saw Farkas, a half-giant with a giant's maul. A red-bearded one, Penda, laughed that nobody would fight him, he was too small, but he could fight one of their children instead: Tostig's boy.

<img src="images/S63 Tostig's Boy.webp" alt="A young blond giant with a fence-post over his shoulder, grinning, carts and giants behind him" class="content-image">

Farkas accepted. The boy, about fifteen feet tall, came out with a fence-post over his shoulder, grinning. Simkin gave three to one on the boy. The bout runs until one yields or goes down.

**Farkas beat Tostig's boy one on one.** The boy went down flat on his back with his fence-post split beside him, and the whole ring roared. Wynflæd the bone-setter came out to see to him and found nothing broken.

<img src="images/S63 Tostig.webp" alt="Tostig, a huge fair-bearded giant in rough wool and fur" class="content-image">

Farkas told Tostig where the maul came from and his whole story: the Skyfather's Reckoning, carried east by his mother Yrga's line; his father Hadrik, the human who beat Yrga in a witnessed unarmed contest; Thurr'gath; and the company's world. Tostig sat down cross-legged in the dirt to listen, and giants nearby stopped to listen too. He liked the part about Hadrik best. Of the giants' kingdoms falling he said it sounded like a lie, but Farkas didn't look like a liar. He knew the maul for western giant work and sent Farkas west to the kingdoms' forges, where the smiths can read the marks.

Farkas asked about the smith in Hrothgar's kingdom. Tostig didn't know the smith's name; he had never been there, it was too far. He knew Hrothgar's city, **Phlegre**, by its forges, which never go out and glow along the foot of the city at night, out west on the high plains. He also knew Ceolric, Hrothgar's warleader, who comes east to fight on the field twice a year because Hrothgar gives him no war. He told Farkas to go to Phlegre and ask there for the smith who reads the marks.

### To Lemnor

<img src="images/S63 Lemnor.webp" alt="A hill town of low stone houses and red roofs among terraces, steam rising from the streets" class="content-image">

The company travelled three days south-east into warm hill-country to **Lemnor**, where the Bastion of Fireblood will be. The hills were warm: frost on the grass at dawn, but by mid-morning the ground was warm as a hearthstone, steam came out of cracks in the hillsides, and terraced vines and orange trees stood green in winter. Lemnor is dwarf country: hot springs and warm ground, forges dug down to where the earth is hottest, and low thick-walled towns with steam coming up through the street grates and the ring of hammers coming up through the paving.

<img src="images/S63 Fabrissa.webp" alt="Fabrissa, a young copper-haired dwarf smith with a thick plait, at her anvil" class="content-image">

They came into the town in the afternoon. In the square the young smith Fabrissa, copper-haired with a plait as thick as her arm, was testing a plain grey blade of her own against one of Gaucelm's bronze ones in front of a crowd of laughing dwarves. Among the soldiers of the levies leaning on a wall to watch was an old dwarf paladin in a coat of bronze scale.

Veezara stepped in between Fabrissa and Gaucelm before she struck. It was only a scuffle between smiths. Gaucelm and Fabrissa said they didn't want Kaedris showing up over it. Veezara asked who Kaedris was and where he could find her, and Gaucelm, surprised that anyone could ask, told him she was the war-captain and pointed him to the west of Lemnor, where the levies' barracks stand.

### The barracks

<img src="images/S63 Sicart.webp" alt="Sicart, an old white-bearded dwarf in a coat of bronze scale, a hammer at his side" class="content-image">

The company went west after the old paladin and caught up with him at the barracks: a long squat block of dark stone with slit windows and steam rising from a grate at each corner, with forty or so dwarves and a few men drilling in bronze scale in the yard out front. He was **Sicart**, the Oath-Smith. Every scale of his coat had a little seal struck into it.

Veezara told Sicart that his powers weren't working. Sicart asked when they had gone, and told him that a paladin's power going out in his hand means a broken oath. He showed him the newer scale high on his own chest, where he had cut out an oath he once broke, and told him the way back in Lemnor: say aloud to a witness what he failed to do, then go down to the deep forge and swear the oath again on hot iron.

### The deep forge

<img src="images/S63 The Deep Forge.webp" alt="A cavern whose rock glows orange with heat, a slab of glowing stone with iron laid on it and no fire anywhere" class="content-image">

Veezara went down with Sicart by a steep, low stair cut for dwarves to the deep forge under Lemnor, where the rock itself glows orange with the earth's heat and iron is worked with no fire. Its smith, Peire, a deaf old dwarf with a black beard, grumbled that Sicart had said he was done bringing them down, and let them use the slab. Sicart set a blank bronze scale on the hot stone and put a striking-punch and a hammer in Veezara's reach.

At the slab Veezara told Sicart what happened to Talaris at Epidaure, that he stood by while Malcius burned him, and gave his reasoning. Peire heard it too. Sicart heard him out without judging and told him to take up the hammer and swear.

Veezara swore his oath again in his own words and struck it into the bronze scale. The hammer rang off the punch with one clean high note. The bronze took the mark and flared bright gold, and **his divine powers came back.** Sicart threaded the struck scale on a cord and gave it to him to keep.

Anvil asked Peire what he was made of. Peire walked all the way round him, rapped a plate and listened to the ring. The frame is ordinary iron, plainly worked, and the amber-glowing pieces in his chest are **orichalc**. Peire knows the metal and has worked it; it is dear, but nothing strange to him.

### Kaedris

<img src="images/S63 Kaedris.webp" alt="Kaedris, a tall red-haired woman in bronze-faced armour and a red cloak, arms folded" class="content-image">

Back up in the barracks yard Kaedris was walking the drilling levies, a tall human woman of about forty with short red-brown hair and a scar through one eyebrow, in a linen cuirass faced with bronze scale and a red cloak, calling each soldier by name. Her one-handed sergeant Guilhem, with a shield strapped to the stump of his arm, walked beside her. Sicart brought the company to her.

<img src="images/S63 Guilhem.webp" alt="Guilhem, a grizzled bald sergeant in bronze scale with a red cloak and a round shield" class="content-image">

Veezara asked Kaedris about the war in the north. She told him what the south knows of it: Tiryn and Lycene have fought over the chalk uplands between them for longer than anyone remembers, with burned sheepfolds and hired companies. It's their war and nobody else's, and Lemnor's levies have never marched to it. What started it, nobody in the south knows and nobody in the north agrees: ask a man of Tiryn and Lycene started it, ask a man of Lycene and it was Tiryn.

Veezara gave Kaedris his name. She repeated it, then asked the others for theirs; Karusa gave hers.

Veezara fought Kaedris in the yard in front of the levies, who made a ring without being told, and **she beat him.** He was at 0 hit points, and she gave him a potion of healing in a small clay flask, which he drank. Half the yard banged their shields and shouted "Mam!"

Veezara got up and told her it was a good duel. She agreed and asked again what had brought them to Lemnor. Then she told the levies in training to run laps around the barracks, and said she would treat them at the tavern afterwards, which raised their spirits.

### Gaucelm's workshop

<img src="images/S63 Gaucelm.webp" alt="Gaucelm, a chestnut-bearded dwarf smith in a red tunic with a bronze blade, in a shop hung with weapons" class="content-image">

Farkas went to see what Lemnor's forges had to sell, and found Gaucelm's workshop still open in the evening: the forges are underground and never go cold, so the street of the weapon-smiths stays open after dark. Gaucelm knew the maul for giant work at a glance, and showed Farkas his racks of fire-forged bronze arms.

Farkas handed Gaucelm the three facets from the Mnemon Hart the company killed in the Idea-Dunes, smoky red, bruised violet and cold blue, and asked what he could do with them. Held up to the lamp, each had something moving inside it. Gaucelm agreed to set them in a new longsword he would forge himself, with the three facets in a bronze ring at the guard. Because the facets come from the company's own world, the finished sword will come home through the portal. The sword is named **Mind-Me**: in Lemnor's speech, to *mind* a thing is to remember it.

<img src="images/S63 Mind-Me.webp" alt="A leaf-bladed bronze longsword with three coloured crystals set in a ring at the guard" class="content-image">

### The Hot Spring

<img src="images/S63 The Hot Spring.webp" alt="A long low bath-house hall round a sunken pool of milky blue water, steam and lamplight" class="content-image">

For the night the company took rooms at the Hot Spring, the bath-house inn on the square, built over a spring: a long low stone hall round a sunken pool of milky blue water, with steam rolling out from under its eaves. The bath-keeper, Esclarmonda, had already heard about the yard. The rooms at the back have warm stone floors and need no fire. They long rested there.

### The outskirts

<img src="images/S63 Suture Pilgrim.webp" alt="A pale, stitched, many-limbed figure bent over the grass, sewing a seam in the ground in a green valley" class="content-image">

In the morning the company went out to explore Lemnor's outskirts: the steaming vent fields on the hillsides, the hot springs and the terraces. Out there they saw a Suture Pilgrim at its work. They tried to talk to it, realised it didn't speak, and left it to its work.

### The coral coast

<img src="images/S63 The Coral Coast.webp" alt="A white coral beach on a turquoise sea with marsh-boats drawn up and dark islands on the horizon" class="content-image">

The company set out south, past the edge of the lizardfolk marshes, through reed-channels and past stilt-houses, to the coral coast. The sea was warm even in winter, clear turquoise over pink and gold coral, with dark isles on the horizon across the warm strait under a haze of dusk. Lizardfolk fishermen were hauling nets on the beach, their flat marsh-boats drawn up on the sand.

<img src="images/S63 The Old Fisherman.webp" alt="An old grey-crested lizardfolk fisherman hauling a net on a white beach" class="content-image">

Veezara went over to them. The old grey-crested one left the net and met him: he had heard of gold scales but never seen them on land. Veezara bought two fish from him for a silver piece. Then the company crossed the strait.

### Acheront

<img src="images/S63 Acheront.webp" alt="A twilight city of grey houses on canals, black boats on still water and lamps at half-light" class="content-image">

Across the strait was **Acheront**, the old city of the Shadar-Kai on the ground where the Blackfold will stand. It was free, in its own twilight, under its queen. The light didn't go dark as they came in; it went grey, a soft even dusk that stays. The city rose in tiers over a web of canals, with long low black boats on the water and every lamp turned to exactly half-light; a woman with a long brass hook walked the quay adjusting each one. Every doorstep had a threshold-stone worn hollow in the middle. The Shadar-Kai here are written on: lines of fine raised scars on their arms, throats and faces, a few on the children and so many on the old that there is barely skin between them.

<img src="images/S63 The Girl from the Harbour.webp" alt="A young white-haired Shadar-Kai woman leaning forward and staring, a canal bridge behind her" class="content-image">

A girl of about nineteen, Ebbe, followed the company up from the harbour, staring at their unmarked arms. Veezara turned and confronted her on a canal bridge. She saw that Karusa, a grown Shadar-Kai woman, had no marks, and ran off to fetch the midwife.

<img src="images/S63 Ursula.webp" alt="Ursula, an older silver-haired Shadar-Kai midwife with scarred forearms, in a lamplit room" class="content-image">

The midwife, **Ursula**, cuts the identity marks on Acheront's newborns. She came back with Ebbe, saw Karusa unmarked, took her wrist and turned it to the light. She had cut the first marks on every child born in the city these forty years, she said, and asked whose child Karusa was. Karusa answered only "Nobody's." Ursula offered to cut her identity marks, with a name of her own choosing, and Karusa agreed.

<img src="images/S63 Ursula's House.webp" alt="A midwife's lamplit room with a cradle, pigment pots on a shelf and fine blades laid out on a table" class="content-image">

The company followed Ursula up a stair off the canal-side to her house, half birthing-room and half kitchen, with a cradle by the hearth and pots of pigment on a shelf. Ursula laid out her blades and told Karusa what the first marks say: her given name, her mother's line and her island of birth. She asked for all three before she cut.

Karusa answered: "Karusa. No mother. No island." Ursula cut her name, Karusa, into the inside of her left wrist, and left the two places for mother and island bare, because she would not cut what wasn't true; an empty place, she said, is not a lie. Karusa watched every stroke go in. Ursula washed the cut with salt water, rubbed in grey pigment, bound it and told her to keep it dry three days. Karusa now carries her first identity mark, her name, and it goes home with her. Every Shadar-Kai in Acheront can now read it.

The session ended at Ursula's house in the half-light, with Karusa's wrist newly written and bound.
