# Session 60, One Thing Each
<!-- cover: images/S60 Tortoise.webp -->

## Session Overview

- **Party Level:** 12 (Karusa 13)
- **Location:** the Blackfold (the Mirror Quarter, the reading room, the Grey Pot, the Shadow Pits) → back over the bridge at the neck → Crownfall and the Green Door → the Ashford road and the edge of the Burn → Mirepost and Spore Gate → a lizardfolk fort → a dark lake → the Pallor → the Marrow & Mead → the Right Hand and the Palm
- **Party Members:** Farkas, Veezara, Viktor, plus Karusa
- **PC Deaths:** None
- **Theme:** Somebody pays for Veezara's face, the face trade comes through the window, and the company leaves the Isles after a bucket of black water goes out into the street. Four legends from the Dealer send them east through the Rot to the Right Hand, where a walking tortoise lets each of them take one thing.
- **Date:** ~late Gorravane, 2,836 AS
- **XP:** Farkas +5,350; Veezara and Viktor +3,350 each

## Session Log

### The Mirror Quarter

At the Lisk bell the Public Shadow Court let out and its people came down the steps into the square. Veezara was on the court square. He felt he was being watched, but couldn't tell from where.

### Stopped outside the reading room

<img src="images/S60 Elf-Faced Man.webp" alt="A man wearing a handsome young elf's face, his shadow on the wall behind him measuring with its hands" class="content-image">

Veezara crossed the city to the Gray Market's reading room and paid his two silver to sit. He asked for a book on urban legends, and the keeper brought him a thin, salt-stained sailor's book, *Things Said on the Quay*, with passages underlined in pencil. What it said is written up in Shadow Law and the Isles' History. One piece of advice was underlined twice: lay a line of salt across your door before you sleep ashore, because the things that crawl under doors won't cross it.

When he came out into the quiet side arcade, two men stepped in from either end of it, one ahead of him and one behind, with their hands empty and where he could see them. The one in front wore a handsome young elf's face with blond hair tied back, a good dark coat and grey gloves, and his voice was twenty years older than his face. The big one behind wore a heavy docker's face with a laced seam up the back of his neck.

> “Somebody's paid for your face. Give it up and that's the end of it. If you won't, we'll take it while you sleep. Your friends sleep too.”<br>— the man in the elf's face

On the lit wall behind him, his shadow held its hands up, measuring the width of Veezara's jaw the way a tailor measures for a coat.

### Back to the Grey Pot

<img src="images/S60 The Bailiff.webp" alt="The Bailiff: an empty livery coat with white gloves, a gold half-mask over nothing, and a caged violet lantern on a pole" class="content-image">

Veezara walked back across the city to the Grey Pot, arriving as the Nera bell rang and the lamplighters went round the Quay.

Soon after, the Bailiff came in at the front door: an empty livery coat with white gloves at the ends of its sleeves, a gold half-mask over a shimmer where a face should be, the Court's seal on a chain, and a caged violet lantern on a pole. The room went quiet, and two guests at the near table found somewhere else to look. He told the innkeeper he was on the food office's round for the Court and needed the kitchen and the service door. The innkeeper lifted the counter flap and let him through, and the violet light went past the tables with the coat's own shadow trailing behind it on the wall. He finished in the kitchen, nodded once and left.

### The Shadow Pits

<img src="images/S60 The Lampwright.webp" alt="The Lampwright, a heavy man in a brocade waistcoat with soot-black hands, on the rim of a white pit" class="content-image">

At the Gorrow bell Farkas and Karusa went down into the Shadow Pits, through the oil store of the lamp-oil warehouse at the end of the Gray Market row and down a stair that smelled of lamp oil and lime. Below was a round pit eight feet deep, its floor and walls plastered white and scrubbed so clean that every shadow on it showed crisp. Two stone steps stood on its rim, each about the size of a doorstep, with a lamp on a brass arm behind each. The tiers above were packed. People ate out of paper cones, and bookmakers chalked odds on black slates and wiped them clean with a wet sleeve. In the warm-up two Shadar-Kai labourers stood barefoot and still on the steps while their shadows wrestled on the white floor.

The Lampwright, a heavy-jawed man in a brocade waistcoat with his hands black to the wrist from lamp-soot, read the house rules off the wall, where they were chalked. On a third step set apart at the far side stood an old Shadar-Kai, gaunt, grey and barefoot, who didn't move at all.

<img src="images/S60 The Twice-Lit.webp" alt="The Twice-Lit, a fencing master between two lamps, throwing two shadows that each hold a blade" class="content-image">

Farkas went to the Lampwright to fight. The Lampwright had to tip his head right back to look at him, and gave him the night's card and purses: the wine lord's shadow (50 gp), the yard shadow from Tetherholm, which owns itself (300 gp), the Twice-Lit, a duellist with two lamps and two shadows (600 gp), the old man on the far step (1,500 gp), and the Spill to close the night (20 gp into the pot). The house takes a fifth.

Farkas took a step, barely wide enough for his feet, with Karusa holding his lamp, and she let it down low. His shadow poured off the step onto the white floor, huge, spilling up the far wall until its head and shoulders were over the front row. People in the front row leaned back, and a bookmaker licked his thumb and changed a number on his slate. The Lampwright took one look and matched him against the Twice-Lit: a fencing master's face, lean and grey-haired with a pointed grey beard, a buttoned black doublet and tall boots, gloved hands held behind his back. Two lamp-men hung a lamp either side of his step, and he threw two shadows across the white, both in a fencer's guard, both holding a long thin blade that wasn't in his hands. He nodded across the pit at Farkas, politely, like a man at a dinner.

<img src="images/S60 Old Champion.webp" alt="An old, gaunt Shadar-Kai standing barefoot and motionless on a step above the white pit" class="content-image">

**Farkas's shadow beat the Twice-Lit.** The tiers made a proper noise for the first time that night. The fencing master gave Farkas the same small nod as before, stepped down and went up the far stair. The Lampwright paid the purse, 600 gp less the house's fifth: **480 gp**. Across the pit, the old man still hadn't moved. Farkas took the purse and left.

He and Karusa walked back over the black bridges, since the cheap boats had stopped at first dark. On the second bridge two collectors with a hooded lantern had stopped a Shadar-Kai carrying a crate. "Soles," one said, and the man put the crate down and lifted his foot to the lantern. Farkas and Karusa passed within arm's reach, Karusa under her hat, and the collectors didn't look up. They reached the Grey Pot around the Thole bell.

### Meanwhile at the Grey Pot

While Farkas and Karusa were at the Pits, Veezara stayed at the inn, visibly sweating and terrified of being ambushed in the night. He told Viktor so.

> “Try not to get killed.”<br>— Viktor

Viktor patted him on the shoulder and went to sleep in his own room at midnight.

### The face crew

When Farkas came back, Veezara pleaded with him to let him sleep in his room. Farkas was tired and didn't much care, but he let him. Veezara and Karusa slept in Farkas's room instead of Veezara's, waiting for the face crew. It was a small room, built for a smaller guest, with one lamp, a mirror under its black cloth, two locks on the door (one for the door, one for the room's cast shadow), and a gap under the door two fingers wide. Karusa took her hat off for the night, and her own face came back, grey and close-shorn, for the first time since the bridge.

<img src="images/S60 Patched Face.webp" alt="A man-shaped thing in a patched, stitched face, and a hide lying folded on the floor" class="content-image">

Veezara asked the innkeeper for some salt. The innkeeper was confused, but gave it to him anyway, and Veezara sprinkled it under the door. Nothing came in that way. After the Ulme bell, the dead hour, the face crew came in through the window instead. The latch lifted from outside, slowly, a grey-gloved finger working it through the gap, and cold air came in off the canal. The elf's face came over the sill first, then the big docker, ducking his head, and after them, low along the wall, a man-shaped thing in a patched face that didn't blink when it should, moving like a coat being dragged.

**Farkas, Veezara and Karusa beat them.** The man in the elf's face and the thing in the patched face were killed. The big docker escaped, and he had seen Karusa without her hat.

On the elf-faced man they found a maskwright's brass fitting tag on a cord, stamped *P. LIGHTFOOT · MASKWRIGHT · THE ROW* on one side and *FITTER · No. 3* on the other; a purse of 85 gp and some silver; his grey kid gloves; a pair of Dimensional Shackles in blackened iron, lined with grey leather stamped S.K., with rows of tally scratches inside one cuff; and a small stoppered jar of skin-dressing oil. The face he wore, a young elf's, carried no licence number.

The house had emptied after last call at midnight, and nobody came. The body lay on the boards, and the patched thing's hide lay folded against the wall with its face beside it. Veezara closed the window and salted it.

The dead Skin-Walker had left a black residue on the floor, and the company ignored it until morning. By then it had steeped into the floor and needed heavy scrubbing. Veezara asked the innkeeper for a bucket, and the innkeeper, confused again, obliged. Veezara tore a piece off the curtain and scrubbed the residue out, turning the cloth black and the water murky, and hid the curtain pieces under the dresser. He asked Farkas to drink the water, and Farkas refused, for obvious reasons. Left with no choice, Veezara opened the window and tossed the water out. That drew some attention from people in the street, who couldn't work out why water was flying out of a window. Karusa decided it was probably best if they left the city, so they did.

Karusa reached Rogue 13.

### Leaving the Isles

<img src="images/S60 Bertram Cole.webp" alt="Bertram Cole, a man split down the middle, frost on one half and glowing iron cracks on the other, with a double-headed axe of ice and iron" class="content-image">

They left by boat that morning, and the body stayed in the room. At the registry desk the clerk held each of their shadows against the lit white wall, one at a time, and lifted the bond mark out of it the way you'd unpick a stitch. Karusa stood for it under her hat with her eyes down, like every other foreigner in the line, and the clerk didn't look twice.

As they were leaving, they saw a man entering the Isles who was split down the middle, frost on his left half and cooling-iron cracks glowing on his right, carrying a double-headed axe of ice and red-hot iron. They knew who he was: Bertram Cole, a consort of Kaedris.

The boat took them to the land's end by the black pylons, and they walked back north up the arm of land, out of the shade, to the bridge at the neck.

### Gulm

This time nobody was sitting on the bridge. Halfway across, the stone was split and white with frost in one long stripe, and the parapet beside it was scorched black. From underneath came slow, heavy breathing and a smell of burnt hide.

Veezara looked under it. Gulm was wedged on a rubble ledge beneath the arch, as far back against the stone as a body that size could get. His long right arm was gone at the shoulder, the stump frost-blackened and cracked. His face was burned raw down one side, his big left fist was burned and curled against his chest, and two of his four small eyes were swollen shut. He pressed himself back against the stone and asked quietly if they had food.

They asked him why he didn't go and get food himself. It was too far, Gulm said; he would wait. He didn't understand why the fire man was so angry. He had only asked him for food, and the man attacked him. And he didn't want to go back, because they always take his food.

They told him they might come back to check on him, crossed the bridge, and walked back to Crownfall by the East Gate.

### The Green Door

<img src="images/The Dealer (Green Door).webp" alt="The Dealer behind the Green Door" class="content-image">

Veezara went to the Green Door in the Kennels to buy information on legends the company could follow, and bought four from the Dealer at 50 gp each: the Wrong Hand, Certainty, the Meadow Girdle and the Dog-Bone Whistle. Their stories are written up in Four Legends from the Green Door.

### Toward the Blooming Rot

The company set out from Crownfall for the Blooming Rot, north-east along the old Ashford road. They passed the Fissure, the forty-foot crack across the road with living flesh and old bone in its walls.

<img src="images/S60 Char-Eater.webp" alt="A slow mound of a creature crawling along a burned line, eating char, with green growing back behind it" class="content-image">

<img src="images/S60 Mirepost.webp" alt="Mirepost, a trading post built of pale ribbed fungus timber at the Rot's edge" class="content-image">

Near where Ashford stood, the road brought them to the edge of the Burn, where passion-fire burns nothing and never goes out, and rot-fronds grow back over the fire-scars as fast as it burns them. A great slow mound crept along the line eating char, with green coming back behind it. The company went on north. The land turned green, slowly at first and then all at once: grass, then scrub, then a dark wall of forest on the skyline, far taller than forest should be, with a wet, sweet wind coming off it.

<img src="images/S60 Spore Gate.webp" alt="Spore Gate: an arch of pale petrified fungus over a muddy track, discarded masks heaped at its foot, gold spores in the rain" class="content-image">

They reached Mirepost at dusk. They smelled it before they saw the roofs, a sweetness like fruit a day past eating. The post is built of pale, faintly ribbed timber that gives a little under the hand, because it's fungus, and it can't rot because it already has.

<img src="images/S60 Greeting Priest.webp" alt="A lizardfolk priest in a cloak of moss and grass, with lanterns hung from a crooked staff" class="content-image">

From Mirepost the track climbed north into the green until the trees were watchtower-tall and furred with moss. It rained, soft and steady. Then came Spore Gate, an arch of petrified fungus taller than a gatehouse, standing over the track on its own, with outside masks lying in drifts at its foot, some already growing something small and white. Past it the air thickened with spores, a fine gold haze settling on sleeves and eyelashes.

At the first greeting-grove beyond it, a ring of benches all grown from a single root, a lizardfolk Cycle Priest and two attendants offered masks and asked for their fire. They took the masks. They had no fire to give.

### East into the Rot

<img src="images/S60 Fungal Fort.webp" alt="A lizardfolk fort: a wall of woven thorn before a huge hollow mushroom keep glowing green in the rain" class="content-image">

They walked east into the Rot on a path of springy root and leaf-mould. The canopy closed overhead and the light went dim and green, and it kept raining. Fungus towers rose out of the trees like chimneys, hundreds of feet high. Past a crimson flower the size of a cart, with nothing growing close to it and a lone boot beneath it, they reached a lizardfolk fungal fort at evening.

Its wall was living thorn, head-high and woven tight, and a huge hollow fruit-body behind it served as the keep, with smokeless glow-light in its doorways. There were only a few lizardfolk on the gate, and room on the wall for many more: the fort had been short of warriors since the levy. The ground had told its captain that four were coming, one very heavy, carrying no fire. He gave them food and hammocks and asked where they were going, since east of the fort there was only the lake.

They told him about Bertram Cole going into the Umbrasseur Isles. The captain took it as war news. He went down a root-stair into the dark under the keep, and a little later, from somewhere below the floor, came a sound like a lot of people whispering at once: the Ways, carrying the news out. They long rested at the fort, in hammocks slung high under the keep's ribbed ceiling.

### The lake

In the morning the path ran east and downhill out of the fungus forest. The trees thinned and yellowed, and the ground underfoot turned soft and dark red-brown, giving like wet leather. A bitter smell came up, sharp at the back of the throat even through the masks. Below lay a great dark lake, red-brown and bruise-purple, its currents moving in long even sheets, a yellow crust along its shore and nothing growing at its edge. Up close it made a low, constant, sliding hush.

<img src="images/S60 Moss Elk.webp" alt="Great elk with moss and shelf-fungus on their backs and antlers half bone, half wood" class="content-image">

<img src="images/S60 Dark Lake.webp" alt="A red-brown lake moving in long sheets beside a yellow crust, and a small empty camp of tarp and stilts on the shore" class="content-image">

Veezara read the ground: the game trails came down to the water and turned along the shore, and none went in. Nothing drank from the lake. Across the water, where a stream ran out of it clear on the far shore, a few elk were drinking from the stream, well back from the lake: tall as a man at the shoulder, moss and shelf-fungus along their backs, antlers half bone and half wood.

Along the shore they found a small, empty camp: a resin-sealed tarp on stilts, a hammock, a ring of stones that had never held a fire, and two waxed boxes. The first held fresh-cut mushroom caps, each wrapped in waxed cloth with a bone tag naming a dead person. The second held a courier's kit: resin-sealed rations, spare waxed cloth, a glow-jar, and a small purse of outside silver.

### The Pallor

<img src="images/S60 Pallor.webp" alt="The Pallor: pale, still water, a crust of white mineral and old wrecks along the bank" class="content-image">

They went on east, out of the Rot into grey rock and dead earth, to the western shore of the Pallor: pale, still water, a crust of white mineral underfoot, the wreckage of failed crossings along the bank, and the Right Hand on the far side. They took a short rest on the bank that night, and nothing came out of the water.

<img src="images/S60 Eyes in the Shallows.webp" alt="Two eyes on stalks above the silt at the edge of something broad and flat buried in the shallows" class="content-image">

In the morning, at the water's edge, Karusa saw two small eyes on stalks above the silt a few yards out, at the edge of something flat and eight feet across buried in the shallows, and told them not to step there. They walked around it on the bank, along to where the wrecks lay thickest, and then north along the shore toward the armpit, where the arm meets the body.

Over the following days the land on the west rose and closed in until the shore ended in a steep fold of grey stone, where the dead water pooled deepest and stillest.

### The Marrow & Mead

<img src="images/S60 Marrow and Mead.webp" alt="The Marrow & Mead: a long low trading post at the forest's edge, its yard full of mud-caked wagons, a bone-and-cup sign over the door" class="content-image">

<img src="images/S60 Mead Keeper.webp" alt="The keeper of the Marrow & Mead, broad and well fed, in thin leather gloves" class="content-image">

They turned west for a while, back toward the Rot's border, to the Marrow & Mead, a trading post and tavern for merchants who risk the passage between the Unclaimed and the Rot. It's a long, low building of old timber and stone where a track comes out of the trees and runs off into the grey, with a yard full of wagons that look as if they've come through something. The sign over the door shows a bone and a cup. Inside it was warm, smelled of wine, and was busier than anywhere for days.

Its keeper, broad and well fed, in thin leather gloves though it was warm, set out cups of the house red, which glows faintly at the bottom of the bottle. Karusa paid their tab and they left.

### The Right Hand

<img src="images/S60 The Palm.webp" alt="The Palm of the Right Hand: an old green forest, with a moss-grown tortoise the size of a house" class="content-image">

They went back east past the Pallor's still end and climbed the fold of grey stone at the head of the strait, where the arm meets the body. From the top they came onto the high, rough ground at the head of the Right Hand, cracked into long ridges and gullies that all run south-east toward the sea. To the east the land ended in cliffs over a broken grey-blue sea scattered with rocky islets. On the Hand the weather turned without warning: clear and still, then black sky and hail rattling off the stone within the hour, then hot light again, though no cloud had moved. Halfway through a morning, night came, with no dusk and the stars in the wrong places, and an hour later it was morning again with the shadows pointing the wrong way.

<img src="images/S60 Giant on the Rise.webp" alt="A giant of stone standing on a rise, calcified, one eye half open" class="content-image">

They walked down the Hand to the Palm, where the old structures stand whole: a wall of fitted stone forty yards long that simply stops, an arch with nothing on either side, steps that climb to a flat top and go nowhere.

On a rise ahead stood a giant turned to stone. Farkas spoke with it in Giant. For a long time nothing happened. Then the stone groaned, its half-open eye turned down to him, and it answered in a voice like millstones grinding, so slowly that each word came on its own.

> “Keep it. I keep it.”<br>— the giant on the rise

<img src="images/S60 Tortoise.webp" alt="A tortoise the size of a house, its shell grown over with moss and small trees and pocked with openings" class="content-image">

Farkas concluded it was there for a purpose, to guard nature, and bade it farewell.

A tortoise the size of a house was crossing the Palm, a hill on legs, its shell dark and weathered like old bark, grown over with moss and crooked little trees and pocked with openings, some of them hacked wider with tools long ago. Through the biggest they could see a space far larger than the shell, going back further than the light reached, with a tree lying on its side, a cartwheel, and a salvager's tent standing inside with a lantern burning on its pole. Viktor tried to talk to the tortoise, and it walked away from him. Farkas tried with *speak with animals*, and the tortoise answered him.

> “TAKE, ONLY 1 TAKE, NO TAKE MORE.”<br>— the tortoise

They caught it up and went into its shell. It allowed each of them one item, and only one:

- **Farkas:** a Belt of Hill Giant Strength.
- **Veezara:** a Tome of Understanding.
- **Viktor:** a Rod of Resurrection.
- **Karusa:** a Vorpal Longsword.

They long rested inside the tortoise, in the lantern light by the tent. It was cozy. The tortoise walked all night, and in the morning they stepped out further south across the Palm than where they had gone in. Veezara gave Karusa the Belt of Dwarvenkind from the dragon's hoard, since he didn't need it.

The session ended there, on the Palm of the Right Hand, with the fingers on the south-east horizon.
