# Session 64: Grey Anatomy
<!-- cover: images/S64 The Hoarder.webp -->

## Session Overview

- **Party Level:** 12 (Anvil 10, Karusa 13)
- **Location:** Acheront, the Long Canal and Demelza's house → the hallway in the wall → the Queen's Hall → the warm strait and the reef → the crossroads where Crownfall will be → Lemnor → the road west → the steading on the giants' high plains
- **Party Members:** Farkas, Veezara, Anvil, plus Karusa
- **PC Deaths:** None
- **Theme:** A doorway opens in a glass-polisher's wall and swallows her son, and in a city where everyone is written on their skin, only the four strangers with nothing written can go in after him. Down a grey stair they find a plaster copy of her house and the boy at a table of faceless things, and at the bottom the thing that has been hoarding it all. Then a queen's thanks and a knife, a sea captain blown off a plank by a dragon, a crossroads that won't let them pass, a sword collected, and a storm bird driven off a giant's herd.
- **Date:** a winter month, carried forward
- **XP:** Farkas +9,100, Veezara +6,100, Anvil +18,200

## Session Log

### Demelza at the inn

<img src="images/S64 Demelza.webp" alt="Demelza, a silver-haired Shadar-Kai woman kneeling by a doorless frame with a rope in her hands" class="content-image">

The first morning in Acheront, at the inn where the company had gone after leaving Ursula's house, a glass-polisher from the Glass Steps came looking for them. She was **Demelza**, in her forties, with glass dust in the creases of her knuckles and her skirt damp at the knees, as if she had knelt on wet boards all night. She had heard from the harbour that four strangers had come out of the south with nothing written on them.

At first light the day before, she told them, a doorless frame had opened in her parlour's back wall, with a cold hall behind it running further back than the house. Her son **Kenwyn** went in with a rope round his waist. She paid out three hundred feet of it, and at noon it went slack. **Captain Ambrose** of the guard went in as far as the first turning, and something touched his arm and took his oldest scar off it. The queen has ordered that none who are written may go in. She asked the company to go in because they had nothing written to lose: *"I have a son in there."*

### The house on the Long Canal

<img src="images/S64 The Long Canal.webp" alt="A long dark canal between grey houses, low black boats and lamps at half-light" class="content-image">

The company went with Demelza along the Long Canal to her house and told Karusa to stay out of the hall; she came as far as the parlour.

<img src="images/S64 The Frame.webp" alt="A doorless frame in a parlour wall opening onto grey, a rope running across the floor into it" class="content-image">

In the ordinary grey parlour was the doorless frame in the back wall, plainly not part of the house, with cold coming off it like air from a cellar and frost on the boards at its foot. A yard back from the frost ran Ambrose's chalk line, with two guards behind it who didn't look at the frame. Kenwyn's rope was knotted round the leg of the heavy table and ran across the floor and into the dark.

<img src="images/S64 Ambrose.webp" alt="Ambrose of the guard, a silver-bearded Shadar-Kai captain in armour, his arms scarred with marks" class="content-image">

Ambrose was there, his left forearm bare where his oldest achievement scar had been. Inside, he told them, every mark on him went blank, and they came back when he stepped out, all but the scar the grey thing took at the first turning. He wouldn't stop them going in and wouldn't go with them: *"Bring me back what it took, if you find it. I earned that."* Demelza gave them her lamp.

### Into the hallway

<img src="images/S64 The Near Halls.webp" alt="A bare grey corridor with a lit lantern and a stack of red bricks on the floor" class="content-image">

Farkas, Veezara and Anvil took the lamp and walked through the frame; Karusa stayed at the chalk line. Inside, a bare grey corridor ran about five hundred paces: floor, walls and low ceiling all the same smooth grey with no seams, and their footsteps made no echo. The lamp lit only half as far as it should. Kenwyn's rope lay along the floor beside them, the one thing in there with a colour.

The corridor opened into the near halls, empty grey rooms joined by doorframes in the wrong places, with mist ankle-deep on the floor and square pillars standing where walls should be. At the first bend lay a guardsman's dropped lantern, still lit, and a neat stack of red bricks the hallway had hoarded from somewhere. In a doorway ahead stood a grey figure that didn't move when the light found it. Two more came into the doorframes on either side.

<img src="images/S64 A Blank.webp" alt="A smooth faceless grey human figure standing in a grey corridor" class="content-image">

**The company killed the three blanks.** Dead, they were no different from alive: smooth and faceless on the grey floor, and the mist closed over them.

### The great hall

<img src="images/S64 The Great Hall.webp" alt="A vast low grey hall on pillars with a funnel in the floor and a spiral stair going down" class="content-image">

The rope led on into a great hall: a low grey ceiling on square pillars in broken rows, stubs of wall standing alone on the open floor, walls too far off for the lamp to reach. Off-centre the floor sagged into a funnel about twenty feet across, with a narrow spiral stair going down from the bottom of it. As they came in the hall swallowed: the whole floor drew toward the funnel like a throat working, and a low growl came up from below. All three held their footing. Then six blanks came in out of the dark at the hall's edges and down the pillars from the ceiling, one headfirst like a lizard.

**The company killed all six.**

### The stair

The stair from the funnel was narrow, grey and spiralled tight, with no rail, and it went down for about three hours in the dark, the growl coming up from below and the steps shortening underfoot each time the hall above swallowed. The cold settled into their joints. Halfway down, Kenwyn's rope ended, cut clean, the end smooth.

### The copied house

<img src="images/S64 The Copied House.webp" alt="A grey plaster copy of a house's rooms, table and chairs all the same smooth grey" class="content-image">

At the foot of the stair stood Demelza's house, copied in grey: table, chairs, plates, the covered dish, the settee and the empty frame in the parlour's back wall, all the same smooth grey as if cast in plaster from a mould of the real one. Through the back doorways another copy had begun, with less in it.

<img src="images/S64 The Blanks at the Table.webp" alt="Faceless grey figures seated round a long grey table like a family, a pale boy at the far end" class="content-image">

Eight blanks sat at the long table in the poses of a family. In the ninth chair, at the far end, sat Kenwyn, staring ahead, his arm blank from the fingertips to the elbow and one side of his face smoothing over. The blank at the head of the table wore his name across its face and called to the company in his voice.

**The company killed all eight.** As each one wearing a mark of Kenwyn's died, the mark slid off it and back onto him, as if being written back in. With the name-wearer dead he knew himself again. His arm and the side of his face were still smooth grey, but the grey had stopped spreading. He asked whether his mother was well, and said he didn't know why he had sat down.

<img src="images/S64 Kenwyn.webp" alt="Kenwyn, a pale silver-haired Shadar-Kai youth sitting in a grey room" class="content-image">

### The bottom

Veezara took Kenwyn back up toward the frame himself. Farkas and Anvil went on through the back doorways, past copies of the house with less and less in them, then only the outlines of rooms on the floor, down toward the growl and a dull yellow light.

<img src="images/S64 The Hoarder.webp" alt="A huge grey faceless creature with red bricks fused into its back and a lantern glowing in its chest, crouched on a heap of grey furniture" class="content-image">

The floor ran down into a low square chamber on square pillars, its walls creased like folded cloth and folding inward. In the middle rose a hoard thirty feet across of everything the hallway had taken, all drained grey: chairs, tables, crates, sacks, rugs, a child's stool, a little rowing boat upended on top. On it crouched the biggest blank of all, twice Farkas's height even crouching, with red bricks fused into its back like scales, Ambrose's lantern burning in its chest, a coil of cut rope round one arm, and Ambrose's long achievement scar on its forearm.

**Farkas and Anvil killed the Hoarder.** It slid headfirst down its hoard, and as it died it said: *"Curse her..."* Everything it had taken left it: the lantern rolled out of its chest still burning, the rope and the bricks dropped from its body, and Ambrose's scar went off its forearm. A glove of its own grey skin dropped from it too: the Grey Hand. Then the creased walls folded in hard, the floor tilted up, and the whole hallway began to pull back toward the frame. They ran.

### Out

Everyone made the run, and they came out through the frame into Demelza's parlour with Kenwyn. Behind them the grey shut, the frame went dark and still, the frost melted across the chalk, and the doorway was only plaster. A handkerchief-sized scrap of the hallway's grey wall came out stuck to Anvil's foot, cold to the touch. As soon as the frame closed, Ambrose's scar was back on his arm and Kenwyn's arm and face returned to normal. Demelza got her son back whole and held on to him a long while. Ambrose said he would stand for them before the queen. Karusa, still at the chalk line, counted them back.

### The invitation

<img src="images/S64 Simeon.webp" alt="Simeon the Skin-Reader, an old white-haired Shadar-Kai man with densely scarred hands" class="content-image">

Ambrose went up to the Queen's Hall to report. Within the hour an old white-haired man whose marks overlap came to the house with two of the guard: **Simeon** the Skin-Reader, who reads strangers for the queen. He brought the company the queen's invitation to her court, to be thanked for clearing the hallway. It was an invitation, not a summons; he would still read them, he said, but afterwards.

### The Queen's Hall

<img src="images/S64 The Queen's Hall.webp" alt="A long grey hall lit at half-light, its walls cut with lines of script, a worn channel down the floor" class="content-image">

The Queen's Hall stands at the top of the city: a long grey hall with doorframes and no doors, its walls cut from end to end with the scar-tongue, lamps at half-light, and a channel worn down the middle of the floor by the feet of everyone who has walked to the queen. The court stood along both walls.

<img src="images/S64 Queen Elspeth.webp" alt="Queen Elspeth of Acheront, pale and white-haired, scarred with fine marks, in a black gown and a dark crown above the twilight sea" class="content-image">

**Queen Elspeth** sat at the far end on a plain stone seat, with **Tabitha** the Scar-Speaker on a stool at her knee, and Ambrose stood by the frames with his sleeve pushed up so the scar showed. Before the court the queen said what they had done, gone where no one written could, brought Kenwyn back to his mother and Ambrose's scar back to his arm, and told them that if they wanted anything, and it was hers to give, she would give it.

<img src="images/S64 Tabitha.webp" alt="Tabitha the Scar-Speaker, a small bent old Shadar-Kai woman covered in marks, at a table of blades" class="content-image">

The company asked for an item. From her own collection the queen gave Karusa **the First Knife**, in a long narrow box of grey wood: the blade the first scar-speaker of Acheront cut the first name with, passed from queen to queen and never used.

Farkas asked the queen about Phlegre. She told him it was the land of the giants, as everyone knows.

Veezara copied some of the scar-tongue on the hall's walls into his book, and Simeon read it for him. The walls carry the queens of Acheront, oldest by the frames and newest by the seat: each queen's name, and beneath it the deeds of her reign. The oldest line, by the frames, is worn too smooth for anyone to read; the court says it's the first name ever cut, the one the First Knife cut. Beside the seat is Elspeth's name and her deeds, and the wall to the right of it is bare stone.

Further back, Simeon showed him the older rulers. The oldest stretch of wall is cut in two columns: Acheront used to have two seats, a king and a queen crowned together and ruling side by side. Partway along, one king's name, **Osric**, has a line cut straight through it: he took the sea-raiders' coin to let them through the strait. The Shadar-Kai don't erase a name, so they struck it through where everyone can still read it. His queen, **Avice**, drove him and the raiders out. After her the king's column stops, and there have been queens alone ever since. The bare stone beside Elspeth's name is where the king's column would run.

### Back over the strait

<img src="images/S64 The Dragon of the Reef.webp" alt="A huge green-bronze dragon crusted with coral lying along a black reef in a turquoise sea" class="content-image">

The company left Acheront to go back to Lemnor, and walked back across the warm strait. Partway across they passed a long green-bronze back crusted with coral and barnacles, lying along a reef of black rock, which they had taken for part of the reef. It was a dragon.

<img src="images/S64 The Caravel.webp" alt="A pale gold-grained caravel with furled sails on a sunlit sea" class="content-image">

Lying off the far side of her reef was a caravel of pale gold-grained wood, sails furled, sitting dead still while everything else on the strait drifted. At her rail a broad, loud captain with one eye milk-white called hello to the dragon, Halcyra, like an old friend, and she answered him, calling him **Mordecai**. Neither of them noticed the company going by. As they passed, the two played a game: the crew ran a plank out over the side, the captain walked to the end, and the dragon blew a long jet of boiling steam that knocked him clean off it into the sea. He came up spluttering, shouted "Missed!" at her, and asked if that was her breath or had she farted. The crew hauled him out on a rope, laughing, and the dragon laid her head back down, pleased with herself.

<img src="images/S64 Mordecai.webp" alt="Mordecai, a broad laughing sea captain in a blue coat and tricorn hat at a ship's rail" class="content-image">

The company went out onto the black rock at the end of the reef to see for themselves. The rock was warm underfoot, still wet from the steam, and crunched with shells. The dragon, who had never seen a warforged or a gold dragonborn, swung her head round and asked what they were. Mordecai, dripping at the rail, wanted to know who they were and offered to guess where they'd come from, the loser to buy the rum.

### Where Crownfall will be

<img src="images/S64 The Granary Lowland.webp" alt="Golden farmland in long strips with vines, windmills on low hills and a cart on a dirt road" class="content-image">

From the strait the company went north across the warm lowland, wheat and barley in strips miles long, vines on the south slopes and windmills on the low hills, to the ground where Crownfall stands in their own time. It was empty: open stubble at the crossing of old roads, a single poplar at a field corner, a crow on a fencepost, and nothing built on it at all.

<img src="images/S64 The Guardian of the Crossroads.webp" alt="A tall cloaked guardian of bronze and bone with a staff, standing at the centre of a crossroads in farmland" class="content-image">

At the crossing itself stands a **route guardian**, the same make as the Old Warden of the Knuckle West Pass but new: bright bronze, white bone-plates, grey cloak whole to the hem, the script on its staff fresh-cut. When they stepped onto the crossing, the glow in its empty hood brightened and a flat low voice came out of the script on its staff: *"The route is closed to the unwitnessed. Present yourselves or turn back."*

Anvil told it his name was Anvil. It said the same words a second time and lifted its staff, the script shining up it line by line. Veezara walked toward it and asked who the unwitnessed were. It didn't answer. He walked away off the crossing before it acted, and it stopped at the edge of its ground and went back to the centre.

Then Karusa walked up to it, and it attacked. **The company ran.** It stopped at the edge of the crossing as if at a drawn line, didn't follow, and went back to stand still at the centre. They went round it through the fields.

### Back to Lemnor

<img src="images/S64 Mind-Me.webp" alt="A leaf-bladed bronze longsword with three coloured crystals set in a ring at the guard" class="content-image">

The company went south to Lemnor for Farkas's sword. At Gaucelm's workshop **Mind-Me** was finished, lying in a long wooden case on a bed of grey felt. Gaucelm counted the 800 gp twice, out loud, and Farkas took the sword. The company long rested in Lemnor.

### West to the high plains

<img src="images/S64 The High Plains.webp" alt="Giant-scale stone buildings and stairs on a windswept high plain under a dark sky" class="content-image">

The company set out for Phlegre by the straight road west-north-west from Lemnor: across the warm lowland, round the guarded crossroads through the fields, past Twin Reach with its yard packed with wagons, and up onto the giants' high plains, where the grass goes up to the sky and the wind doesn't stop.

<img src="images/S64 The Steading.webp" alt="A long hall of huge stone slabs and a stone fold on a stormy plain, black cattle in the fold" class="content-image">

On the road they came to a lone steading: a long hall of stone slabs big enough to stable a ship, a stone fold, and a stone table outside. A herd of black aurochs the size of houses had broken out across the road with a storm coming in, and Hereward of the Long Knife, the giant of the steading, was rounding them up with his sons. At his belt hung a long knife of dark iron, longer than a man, with clan-marks scarred into the base of the blade.

<img src="images/S64 The Giant with the Long Knife.webp" alt="A huge bearded giant in fur and wool with a great knife at his belt, black aurochs behind him" class="content-image">

He asked them to help get the aurochs in before the storm and the bird that rides it, which takes one cow every storm. The company helped, and the herd was in the fold with the gate shut as the first rain came down. The bird came with the storm anyway, screaming, and began circling the fold. Hereward drew his long knife to stop it taking a cow this time, and the company fought it with him.

<img src="images/S64 The Roc.webp" alt="A gigantic dark bird with spread wings swooping over a herd of black cattle in a storm" class="content-image">

**The company and the giant killed the roc.** No aurochs was taken.

<img src="images/S64 Aelfthryth.webp" alt="Ælfthryth, a grey-haired giantess in a brown dress with a ladle, at a great stone table" class="content-image">

The giant's wife, **Ælfthryth**, who decides who eats at the steading's table, called the company in out of the storm and served them first. They ate in the long hall with the family, sitting on the table itself because the benches came up to their chins, while the giant told the roc story to his sons three times, the bird bigger each time. They long rested there. In the morning the giant walked them to the gate and pointed them west: three days on the road to Phlegre, walking toward the forge-glow in the sky.
