# Session 58 — Count to Three

## Session Overview

- **Party Level:** 12
- **Location:** Marrowgate → the road north-west toward Cerebrax → Greed (the casino, the grey, the vault in the sinkhole) → somewhere east of Vor'Khalem → Crownfall
- **Party Members:** Farkas, Veezara, Viktor — plus Karusa
- **PC Deaths:** None
- **Theme:** Swallowed off the Cerebrax road and spat out within sight of a casino in another world, the company robs a vault for a grinning cat, gets dropped home far from the road it was taken from, and picks a next road that gives Karusa a bad night.
- **Date:** ~late Gorravane, 2,836 AS
- **XP:** +7,000 each

---

## Session Log

### A Bad Read

Before they left Marrowgate, Viktor sat down in his room with the Abyssal book again. He failed badly, and he carried two levels of exhaustion out of the city with him.

### The Cerebrax Road

They left Ossathar by the gate on its south side and turned north-west for Cerebrax. The first night they camped, took a short rest and kept going. The second day was all road. By dark they were roughly halfway, so they stopped for another short rest and set watches.

On the third watch, Farkas sensed something. Then everything went dark.

### The Casino

<img src="images/S58 Casino.webp" alt="A gold-lit casino above marble steps, under a ceiling of black rock" class="content-image">

They came round on a flat grey plain with all their kit still with them. There was no sky. A black stone ceiling stretched off further than anyone could see, over ash-coloured dust, pale bare trees and far-off grey waterfalls. The only colour anywhere was a building in the distance, all gold-lit columns and windows, so they walked to it.

Halfway up its steps stood a bull-headed doorman in a brass-buttoned greatcoat with an iron bar over his shoulder. He told them weapons went in the counters to the right as they went in. Farkas didn't want to hand his over, so the doorman offered to hold it for him. Farkas took one look at the size of him and agreed.

### The Lobby

<img src="images/S58 Primero.webp" alt="A blue-grey tiefling host in a white dinner jacket, with a serpent's tail and a scorpion sting" class="content-image">

Inside was black-and-white marble and gold on anything that would hold it, with a grand stair up to a set of shut doors and curtained doorways all round the room in seven colours. At the cashier's cage, a grey tiefling chained by the wrist to her own counter was pushing a man's eleven coins back at him because he'd said twenty.

The host found them before they'd taken ten steps. He was a slim blue-grey tiefling in a white dinner jacket from the waist up and a long snake from the waist down, with a scorpion's sting curled over his shoulder. He introduced himself as Primero, took the rest of their weapons and handed them to one of the workers behind a counter, who put them in a box. Then he explained that chips were gold only, one for one, and pointed out the rooms by colour: gold for the wheel, green for cards, red for dice, blue for the bar, orange for dining, pale blue for the machines, and violet, which was private.

### The Dice Pit

<img src="images/S58 Dice Pit.webp" alt="The Dice Pit: red felt tables and a giant iron dice cage" class="content-image">

With their weapons boxed, they bought their chips at the cashier's cage, and Viktor and Karusa went through the red curtain. The Dice Pit was hot, loud and red, with a giant iron dice cage at the back. At the nearest table a huge demon, black-skinned with orange glowing through the cracks and his wrists chained together, lost a roll and slammed both fists on the rail. The pit boss, a scarred tiefling with iron-capped horns, came and stood behind him.

> “Brelan. On the table. Not on my rail.”<br>— the pit boss

<img src="images/S58 Brelan.webp" alt="Brelan, a lava-cracked demon with chained wrists" class="content-image">

Viktor played the iron cage. He called four; it came up 2, 5, 5. He lost twice more, then won one, lost one, won again, and cashed out while he was ahead.

### The Card Room

<img src="images/S58 Card Room.webp" alt="The Card Room: green baize tables under low lamps, with a watchers' gallery above" class="content-image">

The green room was quiet after the dice. The head dealer dealt steel cards that rang when they landed, and she kept one stuck upright in the rail in front of her as a warning. Across the table, a hornless tiefling woman wearing a crown made of other people's horns was trying to buy a man's ring off his finger.

<img src="images/S58 Horned Crown.webp" alt="A tiefling woman wearing a crown made of other people's horns" class="content-image">

Viktor bought chips, sat down to blackjack against the head dealer, and lost all of them: 70 gp.

### A Word with a Starving Man

<img src="images/S58 Starving Lord.webp" alt="A skeletal devil with a too-wide mouth and a sword-length silver fork" class="content-image">

Veezara, meanwhile, had wandered into the orange dining room, where a skeletal devil with a mouth too wide was eating, a sword-length silver fork worn at his side like a rapier. Veezara asked him for a word.

> “An ear. Sure, have one. Sit down, you're blocking the pie.”<br>— the starving devil

Veezara asked where they were and how to leave. It took some persuading, but the devil told him: they were in Greed's casino, and a fellow with a cat's head could be seen hanging about outside who got people out, for a price. His name was Cattermole. The devil added that he would pay for food from wherever they came from.

### The Cat on the Rail

<img src="images/S58 Cattermole.webp" alt="Cattermole: a lean figure in a dark suit with a grinning cat's head" class="content-image">

They collected their weapons and went back out onto the steps. Sitting on the balustrade, legs crossed, was a man in a good dark suit with a big cat's head and a grin that didn't move, even while he talked. He talked mostly in riddles.

> “Four went down the throat, and four came out the front. That doesn't happen often.”<br>— Cattermole

He could put all four of them back on the road where they'd been taken, he said, together and in one go. The price was a job. He gave it to them as a riddle: something that held every name in Greed and had none of its own, lay open all day and told you nothing unless you read it, was chained to the floor, weighed as much as a man, and was kept by someone who hid the whole house it sat in.

Nobody got it. He gave them a map anyway. It was just a line, from the casino out across the grey to a vault.

> “Follow the line. It goes where nobody looks.”<br>— Cattermole

The thing they wanted was a big book at the very bottom, he said, and the doors inside opened for winners, not keys.

### Following the Line

They walked straight out from the casino until its gold was a spark behind them. The grey was soft dust, bone-pale trees and silence.

<img src="images/S58 Sinkhole Door.webp" alt="A round iron door set deep in the wall of a sinkhole" class="content-image">

The line ended at a sinkhole. Little iron grave markers stood in rows along the rim. A narrow ledge ran down the inside wall to a round iron door. They went down the ledge and opened it.

### Behind the Round Door

Inside was a long hall full of curios, every one of them broken, as if nobody had looked after any of it in decades. There was no gold. Grey, see-through clerks who faded into mist below the knee were there, and one of them asked for their number. Somebody said sixty-seven. It wrote it down, noticed they had no tags in their ears, told them to get that seen to, and went back to work.

At the far end, a corridor ran between floor-to-ceiling shelves of jarred eyes. Every eye turned to follow them as they walked down it.

### Three Doors

At the end were three round iron doors in a row, each with a brass face in the middle that wanted to play a game.

The first played higher or lower. They called lower on the seven of hearts and got a king, and the door kept a tooth, painlessly, in a little drawer. Then they called lower three times running (king to ten, ten to seven, seven to five) and it rolled open.

The second played the shell game. Veezara missed the first time and the door kept a year of his life. He found the pea on the next go.

The third played liar's dice. Farkas lost, and the door took his voice for the day. Veezara won, and it opened.

### The Shelves

Behind the doors were aisles of shelving that ran off into the dark, packed with tagged jars of eyes, hourglasses, horns tied with string, stoppered bottles, sealed envelopes and numbered drawers. Grey clerks pushed ladders along them, filing.

One small bottle rattled whenever Farkas passed it. Veezara looked it over without taking it, and when his hand came near it, a round shape under the shelf shifted. Farkas picked it up, pulled the cork, and got his voice back.

### At the Bottom

<img src="images/S58 Hall.webp" alt="A round iron chamber with a giant clockwork safe door and a chained book on a lectern" class="content-image">

The aisles sloped down to the bottom, where the only thing they could see was the book, chained down, with a light shining on it. When Veezara went up to it, the light turned red, and a twelve-foot figure wrapped head to foot in chains of gold tags appeared and attacked them.

<img src="images/S58 Chained Giant.webp" alt="A twelve-foot figure wrapped entirely in chains of gold tags" class="content-image">

Two more of the chained figures came into the fight as well. All three went down. The big one came apart in a flood of spilled gold tags, and the book didn't have a mark on it.

They cut it free and carried it out, taking four things off the shelves on the way: a sealed envelope with a name in it, a memory in a glass marble, an hourglass holding a year, and an eye in a jar.

### Paid in Full

When they got back to the casino with the book, Cattermole was waiting on the rail. They handed it over, and he told them to count to three.

> “You didn't answer my riddle, I did say I would be disappointed.”<br>— Cattermole

On three there was sky over their heads again. They were on a cracked bone-chalk plain somewhere east of Vor'Khalem, nowhere near Cerebrax or the road they'd been taken from.

### Crownfall

It was two days south: slow dark channels and mud-pans, then the North Road past a patrol-cairn and a caravan going the other way, then the plains before the outer walls. The walls came up over the horizon late on the second day. A Toll-Taker at the North Gate wrote down their names and the eye in a jar, without much interest, and waved them in.

They went to the Selvik operation and asked for their bounty, giving Augustin Drey's name. After a while Drey turned up himself and confirmed who they were, and they were handed their quarter for the dragon: 3,000 gp.

### The Night at the Inn

They took rooms at an inn, and the three of them settled on the next road: the Umbrasseur Isles. They put it to Karusa. She listened to all of it and said yes.

> “All right.”<br>— Karusa

In the middle of the night Veezara woke to find Karusa in the corner of the room, shaking. He tapped her on the shoulder, and she turned and attacked him.

> “No... I'm not going back.”<br>“I see now, this was all a ploy... a ploy to take me back.”<br>— Karusa

Veezara held her still with *hold person* so he could talk her down, but to Karusa that only proved they meant to capture her, and his first try got nowhere. The first time he did get through to her, she drove a dagger into her own thigh, trying to get hold of herself.

Viktor heard the noise from another room and could tell something was wrong. He came in to find Karusa, her eyes bloodshot, hurting Veezara, and used *telekinesis* to take the dagger out of her hand.

Veezara kept talking. When he had got through to her three times, Karusa dropped to the floor. She seemed to accept, at last, that they weren't trying to hurt her.

> “I thought you were taking me back.”<br>— Karusa

Veezara told her she didn't have to come with them, and that she was free to do whatever she wanted.

> “Yes. ... It's my life...”<br>— Karusa

After all her years, she understood that she might really be free to do what she wanted. In the morning she was still with them.

### Shopping

After a proper long rest, the first since Marrowgate, Viktor was down to one level of exhaustion. Karusa went out on her own and came back with a hat of disguise and a manual of quickness of action, which she read straight through, since she barely needs to rest anyway. The company bought a bag of holding. Viktor sold his bracers of defence and bought a pair of illusionist's bracers, with 800 gp he talked Karusa into lending him.
