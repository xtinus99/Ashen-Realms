# Session 61: Blood in the Pond
<!-- cover: images/S61 The Red Wood.webp -->

## Session Overview

- **Party Level:** 12 (Karusa 13)
- **Location:** the Palm of the Right Hand → the north-east coast and the island of the Painted Apocalypse, seen from shore → the old yard → the Bloomhollow → the shore below it → the Red Wood → the Pitfall's rim → the Ring
- **Party Members:** Farkas, Veezara, Viktor, plus Karusa
- **PC Deaths:** Viktor
- **Theme:** A thing in an old dry dock grabs Viktor, the Tabaxi of the Bloomhollow give the company a night's shelter, and in the Red Wood Viktor's secret drop of blood brings a man up out of the ground on a chair of roots. What is left of Viktor after that is his patron's, and the company has to kill it. The three who are left walk on to the Ring and wake to a portal outside the door.
- **Date:** ~late Gorravane, 2,836 AS
- **XP:** Farkas and Veezara +5,300 each

## Session Log

### Morning on the Palm

<img src="images/S60 The Palm.webp" alt="The Palm of the Right Hand: an old green forest" class="content-image">

The company stepped out of the walking tortoise's shell into a glade at first light, with birdsong, mist lifting off the long grass, and old oaks round the edge. The tortoise walked away north-west across the glade, its lantern still showing through one opening. From the rise at the glade's edge they could see the fingers to the south-east. To the north-west a thin thread of steam rose where something had cut into a hill, and to the north-east, where the oaks ended, stood the pale trunks of another kind of forest. On the nearest trunk a small gliding drake turned its head, and an eye opened on its belly and looked at them. Further along the glade stood a lone white tree with red leaves, three eyes hanging in its branches, all turned toward them.

### North-east to the sea

The company left the glade and the eyes behind and went north-east to the coast. The weather turned twice before noon: a warm rain that came and stopped, then a cold wind off the sea.

They skirted the pale-trunked forest and came out on the sea cliffs above a sheltered bay where an old shipyard stood whole: long stone sheds, a rope-walk, a dry dock cut into the rock and flooded, and on the slip a pale keel with its first ribs standing. They went on north along the cliff tops, past a lone red tree on the cliff edge with three eyes turned out to sea, and down a gully to a shingle beach facing an island a short way offshore, the island of the Painted Apocalypse.

### South along the coast to the Yard

They walked back south along the shore, over shingle and wet black rock with the sea on their left. A squall blew in warm, turned to sleet halfway across the beach, and was gone before it reached the far end. Then the coast bent in, and the bay opened ahead: a sheltered half-moon of grey water inside two arms of rock, with the yard all round it.

<img src="images/S61 Tidemother.webp" alt="A huge pale cephalopod rising out of dark water" class="content-image">

At the yard the company found a long stone basin cut into the rock at the head of the bay, its sea gate broken and the water in it perfectly still. A tentacle lay on the ground at its edge, barely visible. As soon as Viktor approached, it lashed out and grabbed him, and the fight began: it was a Tidemother. The company fought it until it retreated, out through the broken sea gate into the bay and away to sea.

<img src="images/S61 The Old Yard.webp" alt="An abandoned shipyard on a calm bay: stone sheds, a rope-walk, a flooded dry dock and a pale unfinished keel on the slip" class="content-image">

The company went up into the yard. The ground between the buildings was laid with big flat stones worn smooth, with turf and sea-pink growing in the joints. Every door stood open, and nothing had fallen down, though nobody had been there in a very long time. Inside the long stone sheds, under the smell of old resin and salt, the tool racks were empty, with pale outlines on the wall where the tools had hung for years. On the slipway the keel lay on great wooden blocks gone grey and soft with age, but the keel itself hadn't changed at all: pale, almost cream, smooth as skin, cool and dry though everything around it was wet with spray, its ribs fitted so tightly you couldn't get a fingernail into the joints, and not a nail or peg anywhere. Behind the sheds the rope-walk ran back up the slope for two hundred yards in a dead straight line.

### The Bloomhollow

<img src="images/S61 Bloomhollow.webp" alt="The outer Bloomhollow: tall pale trunks, deep moss and a clear stream" class="content-image">

From the yard the company went up the slope at the head of the bay, following a stream, into the Bloomhollow from the north. Within a few hundred yards the trees changed. The trunks went straight up, pale and smooth as skin, wider than the oaks and with no low branches, only a canopy so high that the light came down in long slanting shafts. The moss underfoot was ankle-deep and springy, and it was warmer than out on the cliffs. It started to rain lightly, straight down and steady, and it stayed ordinary rain. The stream was so clear you could count the pebbles, and low trees along it were hung with fist-sized yellow-green fruit. A hare sat beside the path and didn't run.

<img src="images/S61 Tabaxi Platforms.webp" alt="Tabaxi platforms and rope-walks high among pale trunks" class="content-image">

Then they heard voices, high up. Fifty feet overhead, platforms of lashed wood ringed the pale trunks, joined by walkways of plank and rope, with knotted ropes hanging down to the moss. The grove's Tabaxi were up there at their day's work, spotted and striped and plain: splitting fruit into baskets, mending a net strung between rails.

<img src="images/S61 Abner.webp" alt="Abner, an old jaguar Tabaxi hung with knotted cords" class="content-image">

The company talked with Abner, the old jaguar on the lowest platform, about the country around the grove. He took them for salvagers. He told them that the fruit by the stream is good to eat and the stream good to drink; that of the two grey-green mosses, the soft one closes cuts and the dark one on the north sides of the trunks puts you to sleep; and that the Tabaxi keep away from the dock at the yard, because something lives in it. South of the grove is a red wood with white trees and eyes growing on them. Tabaxi who went in looking for something didn't come back, and some can be seen kneeling in it, so they won't go south of the stream at the border. A blind girl burns the red trees at the border every day with a green sword, and they leave food for her. A man walks the border at night planting the red trees again, and on those nights they pull the ropes up. Deeper in the grove is a part where the Warden lives; it doesn't let everyone in, and few of them try.

Abner let them long rest in the grove, under the platforms by the stream. At dusk the Tabaxi pulled their ropes up. Nothing came in the night. At dawn the ropes came down again and the hunters went out in twos and threes.

### Down to the coast

In the morning the company walked south-west through the grove, along the stream, where the trees grew older and larger. They came to a place where the trees blocked everyone off except Veezara and Karusa. Veezara tried flying over it with Viktor, but the treetops spread to stop them landing inside. Veezara saw it was futile, and they all walked south together instead.

All four turned east out of the grove. The pale trunks thinned, the ground fell away, and the trees stopped at the edge of the sea cliffs. A steep, loose cut took them down to the shore: black rock and shingle strung with weed, rock pools full of limpets and red weed, the tide out, and a line of kelp and driftwood well over Farkas's head marking where the water reaches. A gust came off the sea warm as breath, and a minute later the same wind was cold enough to sting.

### The Red Wood

They went south along the shore and round the headland, and where the cliffs came down to a long grassy slope they saw the Red Wood above them. Red leaves lay on the shingle at their feet, and more came down the slope on the wind. Up the slope the green stopped. Lone red trees thickened into a wood of white trunks under a canopy of red so deep it looked wet, with eyes hanging from every branch on long pale stalks, the way fruit hangs. The eyes didn't turn toward them, but the company was within their sight. The eyes were fruit: the company could see small critters eating them. There was no birdsong. Between the nearest trunks people knelt among the roots with their eyes shut.

<img src="images/S61 The Red Wood.webp" alt="A wood of white trunks and deep red leaves, eyes hanging from the branches" class="content-image">

The company went up the slope to the wood's edge. The leaves were deep and dry under the first trees and loud at every step, and the air was still and faintly sweet, like windfall apples left too long. Inside the first trees were rows of kneeling people on both sides of where an old path had run in, most of them human, rooted at the knees among the roots with their eyelids shut. The oldest were grown over with bark, the roots up their backs and over their shoulders. The newest still wore salvagers' leathers, with their packs beside them: a woman in a salvager's coat with the sleeves rolled up, a grey Tabaxi with a short bow lying beside him, a man with a crowbar still through his belt.

<img src="images/S61 Kneeler.webp" alt="A man kneeling among red leaves, roots grown into his legs, his eyes shut" class="content-image">

### The pond

Viktor walked to a pond in the Red Wood. He checked that no one was looking at him, then dripped his blackened blood into it.

The eyes in the wood turned toward them, and the kneelers opened their eyes and stared at Viktor. Veezara asked him why. Then Veezara asked a kneeler, and the kneeler pointed at the pond, still looking at Viktor. Viktor denied it again while the ground rumbled. In the middle of it, a chair of roots grew up out of the ground, and then a man was sitting in it. The fight began.

<img src="images/S61 Chair of Roots.webp" alt="An old white-haired man in a red-stained robe on a throne of roots, eyes hanging all round him" class="content-image">

In the fight the man in the chair struck Viktor with *feeblemind*, and his mind went. What was left of him reached for his patron, Qhal'nimeth, and his patron took the empty body. Viktor's face went slack and his eyes stayed open with nothing behind them, while the black crystal in his hand murmured louder than it ever had. The black veins stood up under his skin and split it without bleeding, pouring out as wet black threads thick as rope that sank into the roots, the trunks and the ground and pulled tight, hauling him up off the earth into a web strung between the trees. Viktor had become a filthy woven thing.

<img src="images/S61 The Woven Viktor.webp" alt="Viktor's body hung in a web of wet black threads between white trunks in the Red Wood, a dark crystal glowing in his chest" class="content-image">

**The company fought what Viktor had become, and killed it. Viktor was slain.**

The man in the chair of roots had gone, leaving them to deal with what remained. Viktor's Rod of Resurrection crumbled to dust. His body came apart cell by cell, and his clothes were left in the flattened leaves. The black crystal was gone. The company took the rest of what he carried: his books, the Illusionist's Bracers, his coin and his place-token.

### South onto the Palm

Farkas, Veezara and Karusa went on south, down out of the Red Wood. The white trunks thinned and the green oaks took over again, the dry red litter underfoot gave way to soft brown mould, and the sweet smell gave way to wild garlic and wet earth. Then, overhead, a bird sang, and another, and after the silence of the Red Wood the noise seemed enormous. In a long glade of waist-high grass the ground shook, and a giant thirty feet tall, half grey stone and half old weathered flesh gone hard and pale, walked out of the trees and along its path straight across their way. It didn't stop or look down. They stepped back into the trees, let it pass close enough to feel each footfall, and kept heading south.

<img src="images/S61 The Pitfall.webp" alt="A vast forested bowl in terraces, a waterfall pouring over the lip, giant bones in the mist far below" class="content-image">

Through the oaks the ground fell gently, and a low, steady roar grew under the birdsong. Then the forest ended at a line, as if cut, at the rim of a great bowl in the middle of the Palm, so wide its far side was blue with distance: the Pitfall. A river slid over the lip in a long white fall into mist, the forest went down the bowl's sides in four great terraces, each older than the one above, and far below, through the mist, lay the shapes of enormous bones. The ground at the lip trembled for a minute and went still. Evening came early, the sky over the bowl going deep blue far sooner than it should. They took a short rest in a hollow between the roots of an old oak near the rim.

### Morning at the rim

The night passed at the rim, and nothing came. In the morning there was frost on one side of the camp only, a hard white rime with an edge as sharp as a drawn line running straight through where they had slept, and nobody had felt the cold come in. Out past the lip the mist had thinned, and the bones showed through it: great rib-curves, a skull on its side, and further off something like a hand with its fingers curled, each one as long as a ship. Looking down at them, Farkas knew them for Giants of the first era.

### The Ring

<img src="images/S61 The Ring.webp" alt="A seam of white light across a green finger of land, the far half turned on its side, the sea in grey plates, and one small white house" class="content-image">

They couldn't go down into the Pitfall, so they went round the rim of the bowl and on through the oaks, and marched south-east to the Ring. Halfway down the finger it was broken along a seam of hard white light that ran across it from coast to coast, hanging in the air from the grass up into the cloud and humming faintly, like a wet finger round the rim of a glass. Beyond it the far half of the finger lay turned a quarter round on its side, its forest growing out sideways. Where the seam met the sea the water had gone into flat grey plates. A single house of white stone stood on the seam, and along the shore below it lay drift that had come through.

<img src="images/S61 The House.webp" alt="The inside of a one-room stone house: a cold hearth, a pallet, a crooked hearthstone over a dug hollow, claw marks by the door" class="content-image">

They went down to the house. Its door lay flat on the ground outside, its hinges torn out of the frame. Inside was one room, small, stone-walled and slate-roofed, with a beaten earth floor, and the light from the seam came in through the empty doorway white and flat. There was a cold hearth with old ashes and a torn-off metal badge in them, a pallet by the wall with a few rotted threads of blanket on it, the hearthstone levered up and dropped back crooked, deep claw marks in the plaster beside the doorway, and seven tallies scratched into the lintel inside the door.

Veezara searched the house. Under the crooked hearthstone was a box-shaped hollow dug clean into the packed earth, empty, and lying in the dirt at its bottom a small silver holy symbol of the Vein-Mother's church on a snapped chain. Veezara fished the badge out of the ashes and showed it to Farkas. Neither of them knew why it was there. Veezara took the silver holy symbol from the hollow as well.

### The portal

They long rested inside the little house. When they woke, there was a portal outside.
