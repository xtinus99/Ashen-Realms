*Three books from the shelves of the foreigners' reading room in the Gray Market. None was written by anyone from the Isles.*

## The Visitor's Shadow: A Plain Account of the Law

A thin, much-handled book bound in black cloth, its corners worn round by foreigners' thumbs. It's the one ship's people read so they don't get fined. Viktor read it.

- **A shadow is a legal person.** It can own property, inherit, and testify in court.
- **A shadow can commit a crime,** and it's punished through its owner.
- **Contracts need the shadow's consent** as well as the person's.
- **Killing a shadow is punished by death.**
- **Papers and banks:** travel documents carry a shadow print, and banks check a customer by his shadow.
- **Marriage binds the shadows** as well as the people.
- **Death is confirmed when the shadow fades.** Until then, in law, a person isn't dead.
- **A warning,** underlined by some earlier reader: shadows can be stolen, and a shadow can turn on its owner.
- **Where to go:** the Lost Shadow Office for a missing shadow, the Bonding Hall to reunite a separated one, the Public Shadow Court for disputes, and Tetherholm for shadow-tamers.

## An Account of the Shadowed Isles, for Masters and Pursers Trading There

A fat, salt-stained book with a Ribhaven chandler's mark stamped inside the cover, by a trader. Half of it is twenty-year-old prices; the front part is history, more or less. Veezara read it.

- **Before the Sovereigns,** the isles were always twilight, and the Shadar-Kai were the islands' own people, not settlers.
- **The scars.** The old Shadar-Kai wrote their lives on their skin in ritual scars: who they were, where they came from, who they had loved and lost, and what they had sworn. No scar-marked Shadar-Kai have been seen in the Isles for generations.
- **When the Sovereigns rose,** Ultharion took the Isles. The shade became permanent and has never lifted, and since then shadows have had legal standing.
- **The Obsidian Core,** at the centre of the city, is his seat.
- **Advice for visitors:** keep your faces to yourselves, and eat imported food.

## Things Said on the Quay

A thin, salt-stained sailor's book in a cheap Ribhaven binding, the Blackfold's tales as sailors tell them, with passages underlined in pencil. Veezara read it in Session 60.

- **Don't look into a glass after dark.** Sometimes the one looking back climbs out and leaves you inside it. You can tell it isn't you because it casts no shadow, and because it does everything you never would.
- **Tapping behind a covered mirror** at night is someone who went in and wants to come out. Don't answer it, and don't lift the cloth to look.
- **One night a year the shadows go loose.** Sailors stay aboard that night and keep their lamps lit.
- **The man who came back wrong.** A deckhand slept ashore in a cheap room, and in the morning came back to his ship with somebody else wearing his face. The advice after it is underlined twice: lay a line of salt across your door before you sleep ashore. The things that crawl under doors won't cross it.

## Not on the shelves

The reading room keeps nothing on gods or cults. The keeper had never heard of Qhal'nimeth.
