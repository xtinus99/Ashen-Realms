## Getting there

West from the quay over two bridges of solid shadow. A bell rings when a ferry needs to pass, and the whole span folds up out of the way, then settles back down.

## The arcades

Narrow arcades under layer after layer of awnings. Every shop hangs its sign so the lamplight throws a shadow of it onto the street, and you read the shadows rather than the signs: a boot, a key, a pair of scissors.

## The reading room

Down a side arcade, under a sign whose shadow is an open book. It's a small private reading room for foreigners, mostly ship's people and lawyers' clerks: one long, quiet room with shelves to the ceiling and reading tables down the middle. The lamps are fixed low along the walls so that a reader's shadow falls behind him and never across the page, and the one mirror, by the door, is covered. The shelves hold books off the Ribhaven boats, sea charts, law, and travellers' accounts, and nothing on gods or cults.

A woman in a plain grey dress keeps it from a desk by the door. Two silver to sit for the day, name and bond number in her ledger, and nothing leaves the room.

## The Last Lamp

Next door to the reading room, a shop that sells hooded lanterns. Its sign throws the shadow of a lantern with its shutter half closed.

## The Shadow Pits

Under the lamp-oil warehouse at the end of the row, in through the back where the barrels are and down the stairs. The lantern-seller's account: it isn't the man who fights there but his shadow. You stand on a step without moving your feet, your shadow goes down onto the white floor to fight the other man's, and whatever it takes, you take. Bouts are at night, with purses on the undercard and bigger ones later, and everybody goes to watch the old Shadar-Kai who never moves and has been winning for eleven years.
