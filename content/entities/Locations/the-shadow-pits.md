## Getting in

Through the oil store of the lamp-oil warehouse, past ranks of barrels, and down a stair that smells of lamp oil and lime. People go in by twos and threes in good coats and borrowed faces, each with a Shadar-Kai a step behind carrying the lamp.

## The pit

A round pit eight feet deep, its floor and walls plastered white and scrubbed so clean that every shadow on it shows crisp. Two stone steps stand on its rim, each about the size of a doorstep, with a lamp on a brass arm behind each one, and a third step stands apart at the far side. The tiers above are packed. People eat out of paper cones, and bookmakers chalk odds on black slates and wipe them clean with a wet sleeve.

## How it works

The fighters stand on the steps and don't move their feet; their shadows fight on the white floor, and whatever a shadow takes, its owner takes. Each fighter's lamp-man can set the lamp high or low, and a low lamp throws a huge shadow. The house rules are chalked on the wall, and the Lampwright reads them out before a bout. Bouts run at night. The house takes a fifth of every purse.
