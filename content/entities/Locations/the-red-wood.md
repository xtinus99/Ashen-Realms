## From the shore

Red leaves lie on the shingle below it and drift down the slope on the wind. Up the slope the green stops: lone red trees thicken into a wood of white trunks under a canopy of red so deep it looks wet, dropping leaves all the time, a few at once, never stopping.

## The eyes

Eyes hang from every branch on long pale stalks, the way fruit hangs, a few on the young trees at the edge and hundreds on the old ones further in. They are fruit: small critters eat them. They don't follow you. They don't need to; you're always within their sight.

## Inside

No birdsong. The leaves lie deep and dry and loud underfoot, and the air is still and faintly sweet, like windfall apples left too long.

## The kneelers

<img src="images/S61 Kneeler.webp" alt="A man kneeling among red leaves, roots grown into his legs, his eyes shut" class="content-image">

Rows of people kneel between the trunks on both sides of where an old path ran in, most of them human, each facing a tree with head bowed, rooted at the knees, eyes shut. The oldest are grown over with bark. The newest still wear salvagers' leathers, their packs beside them. Abner said Tabaxi who went in looking for something didn't come back, and some can be seen kneeling there.

## The pond

When Viktor dripped his blackened blood into the pond, the eyes turned toward the company and the kneelers opened their eyes on him, and a man rose out of the ground on a chair of roots. Viktor died here. His clothes are still in the leaves by the pond.
